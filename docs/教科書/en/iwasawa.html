From Cyclotomic Galois Theory to Iwasawa Theory

Fix a prime \(p\); climb the infinite tower of cyclotomic fields and read the shadow of the zeta function hidden in class numbers — until algebra and analysis meet in a single line.

Chapter 1The Persistent Shadow of \(p\)

The question at the root of Iwasawa theory is startlingly simple: how does the class number of a number field change as we slowly enlarge the field?

The ideal class group \(\mathrm{Cl}(K)\) of a number field \(K\) is the finite abelian group measuring how far the ring of integers \(\mathcal{O}_K\) departs from unique factorisation; its order \(h_K=\#\,\mathrm{Cl}(K)\) is the class number. Kummer reduced Fermat's Last Theorem to the divisibility of the class number \(h_p\) of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)\) by \(p\), and proved it for regular primes (\(p\nmid h_p\)). Already here the heart of Iwasawa theory appears: what matters is the divisibility of a class number by a fixed prime \(p\).

Iwasawa's idea was to fix \(p\) and study the whole infinite tower \[ K=K_0\subset K_1\subset K_2\subset\cdots\subset K_\infty=\bigcup_n K_n \] at once, examining the asymptotic growth of the \(p\)-part \(p^{e_n}\) of each \(h(K_n)\). Introducing the Galois group \(\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(K_\infty/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}_p\) acting on the whole tower, one analyses the projective limit of class groups as a single \(\Gamma\)-module. The seemingly intractable \(e_n\) are then governed by just three integers.

Iwasawa's class number formula (preview — Theorem 9.6) For a suitable \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension \(K_\infty/K\), writing \(p^{e_n}\) for the \(p\)-part of \(\#\,\mathrm{Cl}(K_n)\), there exist \(\mu,\lambda\ge0\) and an integer \(\nu\) with, for all large \(n\), \[ e_n=\mu\,p^{\,n}+\lambda\,n+\nu. \]

This book derives the formula head-on, in three movements — the arithmetic of cyclotomic fields, the \(p\)-adic preliminaries, and the structure theory of the Iwasawa algebra \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\) — and then carries the reader to the Iwasawa Main Conjecture (the theorem of Mazur–Wiles), where the algebraic invariants \(\lambda,\mu\) coincide with an analytic object: the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function.

To retell the tower of class groups in the language of a one-variable power series ring: that is Iwasawa theory.

The thread of this book An infinitely rising tower of class groups — an object with no obvious grip — is resolved into finitely many invariants. Iwasawa's \(e_n=\mu p^n+\lambda n+\nu\) compresses the entire tower into three integers; the structure theorem of Chapter 8 compresses a finitely generated torsion \(\Lambda\)-module into a single characteristic power series (the configuration of its zeros). And the Main Conjecture asserts that this algebraic resolution-invariant (the characteristic ideal) coincides with an analytic one (the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function). Not which storey of the tower one peers into, but reading the invariants that govern the whole — the invariants set the resolution. This is the viewpoint that runs through every chapter.
Conventions Henceforth \(p\) is a fixed odd prime (\(p=2\) is Appendix A). Write \(\zeta_n=e^{2\pi i/n}\) for a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity, \(\mu_n\) for the group of all \(n\)-th roots of unity, \(\varphi\) for Euler's function, \(v_p\) for the \(p\)-adic valuation (\(v_p(p)=1\)). Rings are commutative with unit.

Part I
Cyclotomic Fields — Building the Stage

Chapter 2Foundations of Cyclotomic Fields

Everything is staged over \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\). This chapter fixes its basic structure: ring of integers, ramification, discriminant.

2.1 Cyclotomic polynomials and irreducibility

The \(n\)-th cyclotomic polynomial is \(\Phi_n(X)=\prod_{\gcd(a,n)=1}(X-\zeta_n^a)\). From \(X^n-1=\prod_{d\mid n}\Phi_d(X)\) and Möbius inversion, \(\Phi_n\in\mathbb{Z}[X]\).

Theorem 2.1 (Irreducibility, Gauss) \(\Phi_n\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb{Q}\); hence \([\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n):\mathbb{Q}]=\varphi(n)\).
Proof

Let \(f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]\) be the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta=\zeta_n\). It suffices to show every primitive \(n\)-th root is a root of \(f\); since these are linked by \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta^q\) (\(q\nmid n\) prime), it is enough that \(f(\zeta)=0\Rightarrow f(\zeta^q)=0\). If not, write \(X^n-1=f\cdot h\) with \(h(\zeta^q)=0\), so \(f(X)\mid h(X^q)\). Reducing mod \(q\): by Frobenius \(h(X^q)\equiv h(X)^q\), so \(\overline f,\overline h\) share an irreducible factor and \(X^n-1\) has a repeated root mod \(q\) — impossible since \(q\nmid n\) makes it separable.

2.2 The ring of integers

Theorem 2.2 \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)}=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_n]\); that is, \(\{1,\zeta_n,\dots,\zeta_n^{\varphi(n)-1}\}\) is an integral basis.
Proof (prime-power case \(n=p^k\))

Put \(\lambda=1-\zeta_{p^k}\). The discriminant is a power of \(p\) only (§2.4), so \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\) is maximal away from \(p\). At \(p\), the prime \((\lambda)\) is the unique prime above \(p\) and is totally ramified, and \(\Phi_{p^k}\) is Eisenstein in \(\lambda\), forcing integral closedness at \((\lambda)\). The general \(n\) is glued from these as a compositum.

2.3 Total ramification of \(p\)

Proposition 2.3 (Splitting of \(p\) for \(n=p\)) With \(\lambda=1-\zeta_p\), \(\ \prod_{a=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta_p^a)=\Phi_p(1)=p\) and \(\tfrac{1-\zeta_p^a}{1-\zeta_p}\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_p]^\times\). Hence \((p)=(\lambda)^{p-1}\), \(N(\lambda)=p\), and \(p\) is totally ramified (\(e=p-1\)).
Proof

Evaluate \(\Phi_p(X)=\prod_{a=1}^{p-1}(X-\zeta_p^a)\) at \(X=1\) to get \(p=\prod(1-\zeta_p^a)\). Each \(\tfrac{1-\zeta^a}{1-\zeta}=1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{a-1}\) is integral, and so is its inverse, hence a unit; all factors are associates of \(\lambda\), giving \((p)=(\lambda)^{p-1}\).

Likewise \(p\) is totally ramified in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^k})\) with \(e=\varphi(p^k)\) — the decisive fact that "\(p\) is totally ramified in the cyclotomic tower."

2.4 Discriminant and ramification

Theorem 2.4 A prime \(q\) ramifies in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}\) iff \(q\mid n\) (except that for \(n\equiv2\pmod 4\) one has \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n/2})\), so \(q=2\) does not ramify). E.g. \(d_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p^{\,p-2}\).

"Only the primes dividing \(n\) ramify" leads directly to "\(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extensions are unramified outside \(p\)" in Chapter 6.

Chapter 2 summary

  • \([\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n):\mathbb{Q}]=\varphi(n)\), \(\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_n]\).
  • \(p\) is totally ramified in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^k})\): \((p)=(1-\zeta_{p^k})^{\varphi(p^k)}\).
  • Only primes dividing \(n\) ramify.

Chapter 3Galois Theory of Cyclotomic Fields

This is the heart of the cyclotomic story: the Galois group is explicit and abelian, and subfields, prime splitting and reciprocity all translate into the arithmetic of \((\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times\).

Theorem 3.1 (Fundamental isomorphism — the cyclotomic character) \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}\) is Galois and \[ (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}),\qquad a\mapsto\sigma_a\ (\zeta_n\mapsto\zeta_n^a). \]
Proof

Each \(\sigma_a\) is well defined (it permutes the primitive roots), \(\sigma_a\sigma_b=\sigma_{ab}\) gives a homomorphism, \(\sigma_a=\mathrm{id}\iff a\equiv1\) gives injectivity, and both sides have order \(\varphi(n)\).

Collecting the \(p\)-power levels gives \(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\), the source of the \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension in Chapter 6.

3.1 Subfields and Kronecker–Weber

Subfields correspond to subgroups of \((\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times\). The complex conjugation \(c=\sigma_{-1}\) fixes the maximal real subfield \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)^+=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n+\zeta_n^{-1})\), of index 2 for \(n\ge3\); this \(+\) (real) / \(-\) (imaginary) split is a recurring protagonist.

Theorem 3.2 (Kronecker–Weber) Every finite abelian extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\) lies in some \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\).

Thus cyclotomic fields exhaust the abelian universe over \(\mathbb{Q}\) — the reason Iwasawa theory is sharpest there.

3.2 Splitting of primes and Frobenius

Theorem 3.3 (Splitting law) For \(q\nmid n\), let \(f\) be the order of \(q\) in \((\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times\). Then the Frobenius is \(\sigma_q\), and \((q)=\mathfrak{q}_1\cdots\mathfrak{q}_g\) with \(g=\varphi(n)/f\), each of residue degree \(f\). In particular \(q\) splits completely \(\iff q\equiv1\pmod n\).
Proof

\(q\nmid n\) is unramified; the residue field is \(\mathbb{F}_q(\overline{\zeta_n})=\mathbb{F}_{q^f}\) with \(f\) the order of \(q\) mod \(n\); Frobenius acts as \(x\mapsto x^q\), i.e. \(\zeta_n\mapsto\zeta_n^q=\sigma_q\). Then \(efg=\varphi(n)\) with \(e=1\).

From this explicit law, quadratic reciprocity follows via Gauss sums (Exercise B-3). "In abelian fields the Frobenius is read off the cyclotomic character" is the bridge later joining \(p\)-adic \(L\)-functions to class groups.

Chapter 3 summary

  • \(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times\) (abelian).
  • Kronecker–Weber: abelian over \(\mathbb{Q}\) = subfield of a cyclotomic field.
  • For \(q\nmid n\), splitting is determined by the order of \(q\bmod n\), \(\mathrm{Frob}_q=\sigma_q\).

Chapter 4Units, Cyclotomic Units, and Class Numbers

Units are the key to capturing class numbers analytically. By Dirichlet, the unit group of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)\) has free rank \(\tfrac{p-3}{2}\). Crucially, it contains an explicit subgroup of cyclotomic units whose index is exactly the plus-part of the class number.

Definition 4.1 (Cyclotomic units) In \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)\), for a positive integer \(a\) with \(\gcd(a,p)=1\), \(\xi_a=\tfrac{1-\zeta_p^a}{1-\zeta_p}=1+\zeta_p+\cdots+\zeta_p^{a-1}\) is a unit (Proposition 2.3). Let \(C\) be the group generated by \(\pm\zeta_p\) and the \(\xi_a\), and \(C^+=C\cap E^+\) its real part.
Theorem 4.2 (Index \(=h^+\)) For the real field \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+\), \([\,E^+:C^+\,]=h^+\), the class number of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+\).
Proof (outline)

Compare the covolume (regulator) of the logarithmic image of \(C^+\) with that of \(E^+\): \(\mathrm{Reg}(C^+)=[E^+:C^+]\,\mathrm{Reg}(E^+)\). The cyclotomic-unit regulator matches the product of \(L'(0,\chi)\) over even \(\chi\); the analytic class number formula then forces the index to be \(h^+\).

"A gap among units measures a discrepancy among ideal classes" — the first appearance of the duality running through the whole theory; Euler systems (Chapter 11) extend it up the tower.

4.1 The \(\pm\) split and the analytic formula

From \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)^+\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\) one gets \(h_n=h_n^+\cdot h_n^-\). The relative class number \(h^-\) is computable analytically; the plus part \(h^+\) is hard.

Theorem 4.3 (Relative class number formula) For an odd prime \(p\), over the odd Dirichlet characters mod \(p\), \[ h_p^-=2p\prod_{\chi\ \mathrm{odd}}\Bigl(-\tfrac12B_{1,\chi}\Bigr),\qquad B_{1,\chi}=\tfrac1p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\chi(a)\,a, \] and \(-B_{1,\chi}=L(0,\chi)\).
Proof (outline)

Factor \(\zeta_F(s)=\prod_\chi L(s,\chi)\) and compare leading terms; the even characters give the real part, the odd ones concentrate in \(h^-\) via \(L(0,\chi)=-B_{1,\chi}\). The constants \(w=2p\), \(Q=1\) (prime conductor) give the factor \(2p\).

The right side is a product of special \(L\)-values: the zeta function lives inside the class number — the prototype of the Main Conjecture (Chapter 12).

4.2 Bernoulli numbers, regular primes, Stickelberger (preview)

Theorem 4.4 (Kummer's criterion) Equivalent: (i) \(p\nmid h_p\) (\(p\) is regular); (ii) \(p\) divides the numerator of none of \(B_2,B_4,\dots,B_{p-3}\).

For regular primes Fermat's Last Theorem follows (Kummer). The irregular primes (\(37,59,67,101,\dots\)) are the real quarry. Vandiver's conjecture "\(p\nmid h^+\)" remains open. The Stickelberger element (Chapter 11), whose group-ring multiples with integral coefficients annihilate the class group (tied to \(h^-\)), is the precursor of the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function.

Chapter 4 summary

  • \([E^+:C^+]=h^+\): the unit index measures the real class number.
  • \(h_p^-=2p\prod_{\chi\,\mathrm{odd}}(-\tfrac12B_{1,\chi})\): relative class number = product of odd special \(L\)-values.
  • Regularity \(\iff\) non-divisibility of Bernoulli numerators (Kummer).
Part II
The \(p\)-adic World — The Vertical Direction

Chapter 5\(p\)-adic Numbers and \(p\)-adic Measures

To control an infinite tower we need a complete coefficient ring that admits limits — the \(p\)-adic integers — and on it the measures that will produce the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function.

Definition 5.1 \(\mathbb{Z}_p=\varprojlim_n\mathbb{Z}/p^n\), with fraction field \(\mathbb{Q}_p\): a complete DVR with valuation \(v_p\), residue field \(\mathbb{F}_p\), maximal ideal \((p)\).
Theorem 5.2 (Hensel's lemma) If \(f\in\mathbb{Z}_p[X]\) and \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_p\) satisfy \(f(a)\equiv0\), \(f'(a)\not\equiv0\pmod p\), there is a unique \(\alpha\equiv a\pmod p\) with \(f(\alpha)=0\).
Proof (Newton iteration)

Set \(a_{n+1}=a_n-f(a_n)/f'(a_n)\); \(f'(a)\) is a unit and \(v_p(f(a_{n+1}))\ge2v_p(f(a_n))\) (quadratic convergence). Completeness gives the limit.

Theorem 5.3 (Structure of the units) For odd \(p\), \(\ \mathbb{Z}_p^\times\cong\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p)\cong\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\times\mathbb{Z}_p\), with \(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p\cong\mathbb{Z}_p\) via \(\log_p\).
Proof

Lift \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\) via Teichmüller \(\omega(a)=\lim a^{p^m}\); the sequence \(1\to1+p\mathbb{Z}_p\to\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\to\mathbb{F}_p^\times\to1\) splits, and \(\log_p\) identifies \(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p\cong(\mathbb{Z}_p,+)\).

The Teichmüller character \(\omega:\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\to\mu_{p-1}\) sends \(a\) to the unique \((p-1)\)-th root of unity \(\equiv a\); \(\langle a\rangle=a/\omega(a)\in1+p\mathbb{Z}_p\) is the principal unit part. \(\omega\) is the ruler that later splits class groups and zeta values into character components.

5.1 \(p\)-adic measures and the Mahler/Amice transform

Definition 5.4 (Measure) A \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-valued measure on \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) is a bounded \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-linear functional \(\mu:f\mapsto\int f\,d\mu\) on \(C(\mathbb{Z}_p,\mathbb{Z}_p)\).
Theorem 5.5 (Mahler) Every \(f\in C(\mathbb{Z}_p,\mathbb{Q}_p)\) has a unique expansion \(f(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}c_n\binom{x}{n}\) in binomial coefficients, with \(c_n\to0\).
Theorem 5.6 (Iwasawa isomorphism — Amice transform) Measures correspond to the Iwasawa algebra: \[ \{\text{measures}\}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]],\qquad \mu\mapsto A_\mu(T)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(1+T)^x\,d\mu(x). \]

This is exactly the transform translating "analysis (measures interpolating zeta values)" into "algebra (power series in \(\Lambda\))" — the blueprint for the bridge in Chapters 10 and 12.

Chapter 5 summary

  • \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\cong\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p)\); the continuous part \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) generates the tower.
  • Teichmüller \(\omega\) gives the character decomposition.
  • Iwasawa isomorphism: \(p\)-adic measures \(\leftrightarrow\) power series in \(\Lambda\).

Chapter 6\(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extensions and the Cyclotomic Tower

Now we define the tower itself.

Definition 6.1 (\(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension) A Galois extension \(K_\infty/K\) with \(\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(K_\infty/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}_p\). The open subgroups \(p^n\mathbb{Z}_p\) give layers \(K_n\) with \(\mathrm{Gal}(K_n/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^n\).

6.1 The cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension

Combining Chapters 3 and 5, \[ \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\cong\underbrace{\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)}_{\Delta}\times\underbrace{\mathbb{Z}_p}_{\Gamma}. \] Fixing the part \(\Delta\) yields \(\mathbb{Q}_\infty/\mathbb{Q}\) with group \(\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}_p\), the cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension; in general \(K_\infty=K\cdot\mathbb{Q}_\infty\).

Theorem 6.2 \(\mathbb{Q}\) has a unique \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension. In any cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension only primes above \(p\) ramify, and for \(K=\mathbb{Q}\) the prime \(p\) is totally ramified.
Proof (outline)

By Kronecker–Weber abelian extensions sit in cyclotomic fields; a \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension is unramified outside \(p\) (inertia at \(\ell\neq p\) is the image of the finite group \((\mathbb{Z}/\ell^k)^\times\subset(\mathbb{Z}/m)^\times\), and \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) has no nontrivial finite subgroup), so it lies in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^\infty})\) and corresponds to the unique \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-quotient of \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\cong\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\times\mathbb{Z}_p\). Total ramification of \(p\) follows by restriction from the total ramification of \(p\) in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{n+1}})\) (§2.3, just after Proposition 2.3).

Remark 6.3 (Leopoldt) A general \(K\) admits \(\ge r_2+1\) independent \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extensions; equality (no "extra" towers) is Leopoldt's conjecture, open in general. We work mostly with the cyclotomic tower.

Chapter 6 summary

  • A \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension is a tower with group \(\mathbb{Z}_p\); layers are \(\mathbb{Z}/p^n\).
  • The cyclotomic one is the \(\Gamma\)-part of \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times=\Delta\times\Gamma\).
  • Unramified outside \(p\); over \(\mathbb{Q}\), \(p\) is totally ramified, and the \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension is unique.
Part III
The Iwasawa Algebra — Algebraizing the Tower

Chapter 7The Iwasawa Algebra \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\)

The algebraic skeleton of Iwasawa theory is the structure theory of a single ring.

Definition 7.1 (Iwasawa algebra) \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]]=\varprojlim_n\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^n}]\). Fixing a topological generator \(\gamma_0\) and \(\gamma_0\mapsto1+T\) gives \(\Lambda\cong\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\).
Proof that \(\Lambda\cong\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\)

\(\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^n}]\cong\mathbb{Z}_p[T]/(\omega_n)\) with \(\omega_n=(1+T)^{p^n}-1\equiv T^{p^n}\pmod p\) (distinguished). Taking the limit, \(\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}_p[T]/(\omega_n)\cong\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\).

Theorem 7.2 \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\) is a complete regular local domain of Krull dimension 2, with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(p,T)\), and is a UFD.

7.1 The Weierstrass preparation theorem — full proof

Definition 7.3 A monic \(P(T)=T^d+a_{d-1}T^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0\) is distinguished if all \(a_i\in p\mathbb{Z}_p\), i.e. \(P\equiv T^d\pmod p\).
Theorem 7.4 (Weierstrass division) Let \(f=\sum a_iT^i\) with \(f\not\equiv0\pmod p\) and Weierstrass degree \(d=\min\{i:a_i\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\}\). For every \(g\in\Lambda\) there are unique \(q\in\Lambda\), \(r\in\mathbb{Z}_p[T]\) with \(\deg r\lt d\) and \(g=qf+r\).
Proof (contraction mapping)

Split \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[T]_{\lt d}\oplus T^d\Lambda\) with projections \(r,s\) (\(h=r(h)+T^ds(h)\)). Write \(f=f^-+T^df^+\), \(f^-\in p\Lambda\), \(f^+\in\Lambda^\times\). Then \(g=qf+r\) requires \(q=(f^+)^{-1}(s(g)-s(qf^-))=:\Phi(q)\); since \(f^-\in p\Lambda\), \(\Phi\) is a contraction and completeness gives the unique fixed point \(q\), with \(r=g-qf\).

Theorem 7.5 (Weierstrass preparation) Every \(0\neq f\in\Lambda\) factors uniquely as \(f=p^{\mu}\,P(T)\,u\) with \(\mu\ge0\), \(P\) distinguished, \(u\in\Lambda^\times\).
Proof

Write \(f=p^\mu f_1\), \(f_1\not\equiv0\); apply division to \(g=T^d\): \(T^d=qf_1+r\). Mod \(p\), \(\overline{f_1}=\overline u_0 T^d\) forces \(\overline r=0\), so \(r\in p\mathbb{Z}_p[T]\) and \(q\in\Lambda^\times\). Then \(P:=T^d-r\) is distinguished, \(qf_1=P\), so \(f_1=Pu\). Uniqueness from degrees and \(p\)-valuations.

Geometrically \(\mu\) is the order of vanishing in the \(p\)-direction and the roots of \(P\) those in the \(T\) (Galois) direction — the two invariants \(\mu,\lambda\) of the next chapter.

Remark 7.6 (Prime ideals) The height-1 primes are exactly \((p)\) and \((P)\) for distinguished irreducible \(P\); the only height-2 prime is \(\mathfrak m=(p,T)\).

Chapter 7 summary

  • \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]]\cong\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\) (\(\gamma_0\mapsto1+T\)).
  • Weierstrass: \(f=p^\mu P u\) (distinguished \(P\), unit \(u\)).
  • \(\Lambda\) is a 2-dimensional regular local UFD, \(\mathfrak m=(p,T)\).

Chapter 8The Structure Theorem

We classify modules "up to a finite discrepancy" — the algebraic engine of the class number formula.

Definition 8.1 (Pseudo-isomorphism, pseudo-null) A map \(f:M\to N\) of \(\Lambda\)-modules is a pseudo-isomorphism (\(M\sim N\)) if \(\ker f,\mathrm{coker}\,f\) are finite. A finite \(\Lambda\)-module is pseudo-null.
Lemma 8.2 Over \(\Lambda\), a torsion module is pseudo-null \(\iff\) finite \(\iff\) its annihilator has height \(\ge2\) (the only such prime being \(\mathfrak m\)).
Theorem 8.3 (Structure theorem) A finitely generated \(\Lambda\)-module \(M\) is pseudo-isomorphic to \[ \Lambda^{r}\oplus\bigoplus_{i}\Lambda/(p^{m_i})\oplus\bigoplus_{j}\Lambda/(P_j^{k_j}), \] with \(P_j\) distinguished irreducible polynomials and invariants \(r,(m_i),(P_j,k_j)\) unique.
Proof (localisation to a PID)

Take \(M\) torsion. Its support meets finitely many height-1 primes \(\mathfrak p_1,\dots,\mathfrak p_N\); set \(S=\Lambda\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathfrak p_i\). Then \(R=S^{-1}\Lambda\) is a semilocal regular domain of dimension 1, hence a PID, and \(S^{-1}M\cong\bigoplus R/(\pi_l^{a_l})\) by the structure theorem over a PID. Lifting the cyclic pieces to an elementary module \(E\) and the isomorphism to a map \(\varphi:M\to E\), the kernel and cokernel vanish at every height-1 prime, hence are finite by Lemma 8.2; so \(\varphi\) is a pseudo-isomorphism. Uniqueness: a pseudo-isomorphism becomes an isomorphism after localising at a height-1 prime; \(r\) is the rank, and at each \(\mathfrak p_i\) the module \(M_{\mathfrak p_i}\) over the DVR \(\Lambda_{\mathfrak p_i}\) determines, by the elementary divisor theorem, the multiset of exponents \(a_l\) at that prime (its length gives only their sum: \(\Lambda/(T^2)\) and \(\Lambda/(T)\oplus\Lambda/(T)\) both have length \(2\) at \((T)\), but only the latter is killed by \(T\)).

Remark (splitting off \(\Lambda^r\))

For general \(M\), the torsion-free quotient \(M/M_{\mathrm{tor}}\) embeds with finite cokernel in its reflexive hull, which is free (\(\cong\Lambda^r\)) since \(\Lambda\) is 2-dimensional regular; thus \(M\sim\Lambda^r\oplus M_{\mathrm{tor}}\).

Definition 8.4 (Characteristic ideal, invariants) For torsion \(M\), \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(M)=\bigl(p^{\mu}\prod_jP_j^{k_j}\bigr)\), with \(\mu=\sum m_i\), \(\lambda=\sum k_j\deg P_j\). These are pseudo-isomorphism invariants.

Chapter 8 summary

  • Pseudo-isomorphism = finite kernel and cokernel; over \(\Lambda\), pseudo-null = finite.
  • Structure theorem: \(M\sim\Lambda^r\oplus\bigoplus\Lambda/(p^{m_i})\oplus\bigoplus\Lambda/(P_j^{k_j})\).
  • Torsion modules carry a characteristic ideal and invariants \(\mu,\lambda\).

Chapter 9Iwasawa Modules and the Class Number Formula

Now we assemble the class groups of the tower into a \(\Lambda\)-module, apply the structure theorem, and derive the formula promised in Chapter 1.

9.1 The Iwasawa module

For the cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension let \(A_n\) be the \(p\)-Sylow of \(\mathrm{Cl}(K_n)\), and \(X=\varprojlim_n A_n\) under the norm maps; \(\Gamma\) acts, making \(X\) a \(\Lambda\)-module.

Theorem 9.1 \(X\) is a finitely generated torsion \(\Lambda\)-module.
Proof (outline)

Finite generation: topological Nakayama, using that \(X/\mathfrak mX\) is finite (only finitely many primes ramify). Torsion: if \(X\) had \(\Lambda\)-rank \(r\ge1\), then since \(\Lambda/(\omega_n)\cong\mathbb{Z}_p^{p^n}\) the \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-rank of \(X/\omega_nX\) would grow like \(rp^n\); but by the control theorem of the next section \(X/\omega_nX\) (in general \(Y_0/\nu_nY_0\) for a finite-index submodule \(Y_0\), Remark 9.2′) is isomorphic to the finite group \(A_n\) (finiteness of class groups) — in general to a finite-index subgroup of \(A_n\cong X/\nu_nY_0\) — so \(r=0\). (No circularity: the proof of the control theorem does not use that \(X\) is torsion.)

9.2 The control theorem

Let \(\omega_n=(1+T)^{p^n}-1\), which cuts out the \(\Gamma_n\)-coinvariants, where \(\Gamma_n=\Gamma^{p^n}=\mathrm{Gal}(K_\infty/K_n)\).

Theorem 9.2 (Control theorem, Iwasawa) If exactly one prime ramifies in \(K_\infty/K\) and it is totally ramified (e.g. \(p\) in the cyclotomic tower over \(\mathbb{Q}\)), then for all \(n\), \[ A_n\cong X/\omega_nX,\qquad\text{in particular } A_0\cong X/TX. \]
Proof (semidirect product)

Let \(L_\infty\) be the maximal unramified abelian \(p\)-extension of \(K_\infty\), \(X=\mathrm{Gal}(L_\infty/K_\infty)\), \(G=\mathrm{Gal}(L_\infty/K)\). Since \(L_\infty/K_\infty\) is unramified and the ramified prime is totally ramified, its inertia \(I\) splits \(G=X\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle\) with \(\sigma\mapsto\gamma_0\). For \(G_n=\mathrm{Gal}(L_\infty/K_n)=X\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle\), \(\tau=\sigma^{p^n}\), the commutator is \([\tau,x]=((1+T)^{p^n}-1)x=\omega_nx\), so \(G_n^{\mathrm{ab}}=(X/\omega_nX)\times\langle\overline\tau\rangle\); killing the inertia image gives \(A_n\cong X/\omega_nX\).

Remark 9.2′ (several primes) With several ramified primes, all totally ramified (true after replacing \(K\) by a suitable layer \(K_e\)), the formula becomes \(A_n\cong X/\nu_nY_0\), where \(\nu_n=\omega_n/T\) and \(Y_0\supseteq TX\) is a \(\Lambda\)-submodule with \(X/Y_0\cong A_0\) finite. The correction relative to \(X/\omega_nX\) need not be bounded — \(X/\omega_nX\) can even be infinite (e.g. \(p\) split in an imaginary quadratic \(K\)) — but \(Y_0\) has the same \(\mu,\lambda\) as \(X\), so the asymptotic form below persists.

9.3 The component computations

\(\omega_n\equiv T^{p^n}\pmod p\) is distinguished of degree \(p^n\).

Lemma 9.3 (\(p\)-torsion piece) \(\#(\Lambda/(p^m,\omega_n))=p^{\,m\,p^n}\).
Proof

\((\mathbb{Z}/p^m)[T]/(\omega_n)\) is free of rank \(p^n\) over \(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\); order \((p^m)^{p^n}\).

Lemma 9.4 (distinguished piece) For distinguished irreducible \(P\), \(g=P^k\), \(D=\deg g\), with \(P\nmid\omega_n\) for all \(n\): for large \(n\), \(\#(\Lambda/(g,\omega_n))=p^{\,Dn+c}\).
Proof

\(\Lambda/(g)\) is free of rank \(D\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\); \(\#=p^{v_p(\det(\omega_n))}\) where \(\det=\prod_\alpha\omega_n(\alpha)\) over the roots \(\alpha\) of \(g\). For distinguished \(P\), \(v_p(\alpha)>0\), so \(1+\alpha\) is a principal unit and, once \(v_p((1+\alpha)^{p^{n_0}}-1)>\tfrac1{p-1}\), each further \(p\)-power adds exactly \(1\): \(v_p(\omega_n(\alpha))=n+c_\alpha\). Summing over \(D\) roots gives \(Dn+c\).

Theorem 9.5 (Iwasawa's asymptotic formula — module version) If \(X\) is a finitely generated torsion \(\Lambda\)-module whose characteristic polynomial is prime to every \(\omega_n\), then with \(\mu=\mu(X)\), \(\lambda=\lambda(X)\), for large \(n\) \[ \#(X/\omega_nX)=p^{\,\mu p^n+\lambda n+\nu}\qquad(\nu\in\mathbb{Z}). \]
Proof

Pseudo-isomorph \(X\) to an elementary module and apply Lemmas 9.3–9.4 component-wise: \(\#=p^{(\sum m_i)p^n+(\sum k_j\deg P_j)n+\nu_0}\); the kernel \(K\) and cokernel \(C\) of the pseudo-isomorphism are finite, so \(\omega_n\) kills them for \(n\) large (a continuous \(\Gamma\)-action on a finite group is trivial on an open subgroup), and \(E[\omega_n]=0\) because the characteristic polynomial is prime to \(\omega_n\), whence \(X[\omega_n]=K\) for \(n\) large; hence every kernel and cokernel in the snake-lemma sequences for multiplication by \(\omega_n\) is determined by \(K\) and \(C\) alone for \(n\) large, and \(\#(X/\omega_nX)/\#(E/\omega_nE)\) is eventually constant (boundedness alone would not suffice) and shifts only the constant.

Theorem 9.6 (Iwasawa's class number formula) For the cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension, \(\#A_n=p^{e_n}\) with \[ \boxed{\,e_n=\mu\,p^{\,n}+\lambda\,n+\nu\quad(n\ge n_0)\,}, \] where \(\mu,\lambda\) are the invariants of \(X=\varprojlim A_n\).
Proof

When exactly one prime ramifies and it is totally ramified, Theorem 9.2 gives \(\#A_n=\#(X/\omega_nX)\); \(X\) is finitely generated torsion (Theorem 9.1), and since \(A_n\cong X/\omega_nX\) is finite its characteristic polynomial is prime to every \(\omega_n\), so Theorem 9.5 applies. In general, use \(A_n\cong X/\nu_nY_0\) (Remark 9.2′) and run the same component count for \(Y_0\) with \(\nu_n=\omega_n/T\) (distinguished of degree \(p^n-1\)) in place of \(\omega_n\): \(Y_0/\nu_nY_0\) is finite, so the characteristic polynomial of \(Y_0\) is prime to every \(\nu_n\) and Lemmas 9.3–9.4 give the same asymptotic form; since \(Y_0\) has the same \(\mu,\lambda\) as \(X\), the formula follows (Washington, Ch. 13). (Finiteness of \(A_n\) alone does not make the characteristic polynomial of \(X\) prime to \(\omega_n=T\nu_n\); in the example of Remark 9.2′ it is divisible by \(T\).)

Theorem 9.7 (Ferrero–Washington, 1979) For abelian \(K\), the cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension has \(\mu=0\).
Strategy

By Iwasawa's analytic class number formula for \(h_n^-\) (no Main Conjecture is needed — Ferrero–Washington predates it), \(\mu(X^-)\) is the sum of the \(\mu(g_\chi)\) of the \(p\)-adic \(L\) power series, and Kummer theory gives \(\mu^+\le\mu^-\); so one shows \(g_\chi\not\equiv0\pmod p\). Ferrero–Washington argue via the non-vanishing of the \(p\)-adic digits of Bernoulli/Stickelberger data; Sinnott gave a structural proof using an independence property of the \((1+T)^{c}\): if \(r_i\in\mathbb{F}_p(T)\cap\mathbb{F}_p[[T]]\) and \(c_i\in\mathbb{Z}_p\setminus\{0\}\) satisfy \(\sum_i r_i((1+T)^{c_i}-1)=0\), then each partial sum over the \(c_i\) that agree up to a factor in \(\mathbb{Q}^\times\) is a constant (independence over \(\mathbb{F}_p\), not over \(\mathbb{F}_p((T))\)).

Hence over abelian fields the growth is eventually linear, \(e_n=\lambda n+\nu\).

Remark 9.8 (Greenberg's conjecture) For a totally real field (e.g. the real subfield \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+\)) and its cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension, Greenberg conjectured \(\lambda^+=\mu^+=0\) (bounded \(A_n^+\)); Vandiver's conjecture is its first layer. It remains open — the central difficulty of the plus side.

9.4 Numerical examples

The smallest irregular prime \(p=37\) has \(37\mid B_{32}\); by Herbrand–Ribet (Theorem 11.3) the component \(A^{(-31)}=A^{(5)}\) is nontrivial with \(\lambda=1\). Other examples: \(59\mid B_{44}\), \(67\mid B_{58}\), \(101\mid B_{68}\), and \(691\mid B_{12}\) (the same prime as the Ramanujan congruence \(\tau(n)\equiv\sigma_{11}(n)\bmod691\)). Use the tools in Appendix E to explore these.

The growth of the \(p\)-part of class numbers is the shadow of a single \(p\)-adic power series with \(\lambda\) zeros.

  • Control theorem \(A_n\cong X/\omega_nX\); component computations give \(p^{\mu p^n+\lambda n+\nu}\).
  • \(e_n=\mu p^n+\lambda n+\nu\); \(\mu=0\) for abelian fields (Ferrero–Washington); \(\lambda^+=0\) is Greenberg's open conjecture.
Interlude
The Function Field Analogy

InterludeBetween Arithmetic and Geometry

To understand how Iwasawa arrived at \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extensions, look at the other world he was watching: the arithmetic of curves over finite fields. There, the formula \(e_n=\mu p^n+\lambda n+\nu\) is a known landscape — Weil's theorem — transplanted to number fields.

The dictionary

For a smooth projective curve \(C/\mathbb{F}_q\), the tower of constant-field extensions \(\mathbb{F}_q\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^p}\subset\cdots\) is a \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension, with Frobenius \(\mathrm{Frob}_q\) playing the role of \(\gamma_0\). The divisor class groups \(\mathrm{Pic}^0(C_n)\) of the layers \(C_n=C\times_{\mathbb{F}_q}\mathbb{F}_{q^{p^n}}\) correspond to the class groups \(A_n\).

Weil's class number formula

With Frobenius eigenvalues \(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_{2g}\) (\(|\alpha_i|=\sqrt q\)) on the Tate module, \[ \#\mathrm{Pic}^0(C_n)=\prod_{i=1}^{2g}\bigl(1-\alpha_i^{\,p^n}\bigr) \] — literally the same computation as Lemma 9.4. The characteristic polynomial of Frobenius corresponds to \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(X)\), and the numerator of the congruence zeta \(Z(C,t)\) is that characteristic polynomial.

The Iwasawa Main Conjecture is Weil's "zeta = characteristic polynomial of Frobenius," carried over to number fields.

Thus the Main Conjecture \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(X^{(i)})=(g_{\omega^{1-i}})\) (Chapter 12) is the arithmetic avatar of the geometric identity. The analogy predicted why "algebra = analysis" should hold; Iwasawa built the missing apparatus — \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extensions, the Iwasawa algebra, characteristic polynomials — by hand.

Where the analogy breaks, and deepens

On the function-field side the eigenvalues satisfy the Riemann hypothesis (Weil); the number-field analogue — controlling the zeros of the \(p\)-adic \(L\) — is far deeper (Mazur–Wiles and beyond). Conversely number fields carry riches absent over function fields — archimedean places, Arakelov geometry, \(p\)-adic Hodge theory — giving Iwasawa theory its own depth. Mazur pushed the analogy to view the arithmetic of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extensions as "geometry over \(\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}\)" (see Chapter 13).

Interlude summary

  • \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension \(\leftrightarrow\) constant-field extension, \(\gamma_0\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Frob}_q\), \(A_n\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Pic}^0(C_n)\).
  • Weil: \(\#\mathrm{Pic}^0(C_n)=\prod(1-\alpha_i^{p^n})\) mirrors Lemma 9.4.
  • The Main Conjecture is the number-field version of "zeta = Frobenius characteristic polynomial."

Analysis and the Main Conjecture

Chapter 10\(p\)-adic \(L\)-functions

Chapter 4 showed that \(h^-\) carries Bernoulli numbers, i.e. special values of the zeta function. Here we lift this family of special values to the whole tower, by constructing a \(p\)-adic interpolating function.

Definition 10.1 (Generalised Bernoulli numbers) For a Dirichlet character \(\chi\) of conductor \(f\), \(\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{f}\frac{\chi(a)\,t\,e^{at}}{e^{ft}-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}B_{n,\chi}\frac{t^n}{n!}\). Then \(L(1-n,\chi)=-B_{n,\chi}/n\) for \(n\ge1\).
Theorem 10.2 (Kubota–Leopoldt \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function) For each Dirichlet character \(\chi\) there is a unique \(p\)-adically continuous (analytic if \(\chi\neq\mathbf1\)) function \(L_p(s,\chi):\mathbb{Z}_p\to\mathbb{C}_p\) (for \(\chi=\mathbf1\), on \(\mathbb{Z}_p\smallsetminus\{1\}\), with a pole at \(s=1\)) with the interpolation property \[ L_p(1-n,\chi)=-\bigl(1-\chi\omega^{-n}(p)\,p^{\,n-1}\bigr)\frac{B_{n,\chi\omega^{-n}}}{n}\qquad(n\ge1), \] where \(\omega\) is the Teichmüller character and the factor removes the Euler factor at \(p\).

10.1 Construction via measures

Using the Iwasawa isomorphism (Theorem 5.6): build a measure on \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) whose moments are the Bernoulli numbers, then take its Amice transform to obtain a power series.

Theorem 10.3 (Stickelberger measure, Iwasawa power series) With an auxiliary \(c\in\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\) that is not a root of unity (e.g. a primitive root modulo \(p\); for \(c=-1\) the right-hand side below vanishes for every \(k\) and the measure would be zero), there is a measure \(E_c\) on \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\) with \[ \int_{\mathbb{Z}_p^\times}x^{k}\,dE_c(x)=(1-c^{\,k})\,(1-p^{\,k-1})\,(-B_{k}/k)\quad(k\ge1;\ \text{and its character twists}). \] For each even \(\chi\) of conductor not divisible by \(p^2\), write \(x=\omega(x)\langle x\rangle\) (after Theorem 5.3) and push the twisted measure \(\chi(x)\,dE_c\) on \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\) forward along \(x\mapsto\langle x\rangle\) to \(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p\cong\Gamma\) (the power of \(\omega\) in \(x^{k}\) reappears as the twist \(\chi\omega^{-k}\) of the interpolation formula); dividing its Amice transform by the auxiliary factor coming from \(c\) (the one responsible for \(1-c^{k}\)) produces an Iwasawa power series \(g_\chi(T)\in\Lambda_\chi\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\), where \(\Lambda_\chi=\mathbb{Z}_p[\chi][[T]]\) is the Iwasawa algebra with the values of \(\chi\) adjoined (\(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\) when \(\chi\) is \(\mathbb{Q}_p\)-valued, e.g. a power of \(\omega\)); for \(\chi\neq\mathbf1\) a power of \(\omega\) — more generally for \(\chi\neq\mathbf1\) of conductor not divisible by \(p^2\) — in fact \(g_\chi\in\Lambda_\chi\), i.e. integral, and this is what the ideal \((g_{\omega^{1-i}})\subset\Lambda\) of Chapter 12 and the invariants \(\mu(g_\chi)\) of Chapters 9 and 14 use; via the cyclotomic character \(\kappa:\Gamma\xrightarrow{\sim}1+p\mathbb{Z}_p\) (the restriction to \(\Gamma\) of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\) from Theorem 3.1), \[ L_p(s,\chi)=g_\chi\bigl(\kappa(\gamma_0)^{\,s}-1\bigr)\quad(\chi\neq\mathbf1),\qquad L_p(s,\mathbf1)=\frac{g_{\mathbf1}\bigl(\kappa(\gamma_0)^{\,s}-1\bigr)}{\kappa(\gamma_0)^{\,s}-\kappa(\gamma_0)}, \] which determines \(L_p\) completely (the denominator vanishes at \(s=1\): the pole of Theorem 10.2).

Two points. First, a single \(p\)-adic function threads through all the special values at negative integers (rational Bernoulli numbers). Second, the "price of \(p\)-adicisation" — removing the Euler factor at \(p\) — is exactly what later meshes with the \(p\)-adic nature of the Iwasawa module.

Both shores are now in place: on the algebraic side \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(X)\) (the characteristic power series of the tower of class groups), on the analytic side \(g_\chi(T)\). Two chapters from now they coincide.

Chapter 10 summary

  • \(L_p(s,\chi)\) \(p\)-adically interpolates \(L(1-n,\chi\omega^{-n})\) with an Euler-factor correction.
  • Measure \(\to\) Amice transform \(\to\) Iwasawa power series \(g_\chi(T)\in\Lambda_\chi\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\).
  • For even \(\chi\neq\mathbf1\) of conductor not divisible by \(p^2\), \(L_p(s,\chi)\) is realised as an element \(g_\chi\) of the Iwasawa algebra \(\Lambda_\chi\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\) (algebraisation); for \(\chi=\mathbf1\) a denominator accounts for the pole at \(s=1\).

Chapter 11Stickelberger Elements and Euler Systems

Two pillars bridge to the Main Conjecture: an algebraic element that annihilates class groups (Stickelberger), and a mechanism that bounds class groups from above via units (Euler systems).

11.1 Stickelberger's theorem

Definition 11.1 (Stickelberger element) For \(F=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)\), \(G=\mathrm{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})\), \[ \theta=\theta_m=\sum_{\gcd(a,m)=1,\,1\le a\le m}\Bigl\{\tfrac{a}{m}\Bigr\}\,\sigma_a^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}[G],\qquad \{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor. \]
Theorem 11.2 (Stickelberger) If \(\beta\in\mathbb{Z}[G]\) satisfies \(\beta\theta\in\mathbb{Z}[G]\), then \(\beta\theta\) annihilates \(\mathrm{Cl}(F)\). In particular, for every integer \(b\) with \(\gcd(b,m)=1\), \((b-\sigma_b)\theta\in\mathbb{Z}[G]\) always kills the class group.

The Stickelberger element comes from the prime factorisation of Gauss sums; it is the finite-layer precursor of the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function and, in the tower limit, becomes \(g_\chi(T)\). Its odd \(\chi\)-component \(B_{1,\chi^{-1}}\) is exactly what enters the \(h^-\) formula of Theorem 4.3.

11.2 Herbrand–Ribet

\(\Delta=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)/\mathbb{Q})\) acts on \(A=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p))[p^\infty]\), giving the \(\omega\)-eigenspace decomposition \(A=\bigoplus_i A^{(i)}\) (\(A^{(i)}=\varepsilon_iA\), \(\varepsilon_i=\frac1{p-1}\sum_\delta\omega^i(\delta)\delta^{-1}\)).

Theorem 11.3 (Herbrand–Ribet) For even \(k\) (\(2\le k\le p-3\)), \(A^{(1-k)}\neq0\iff p\mid B_k\). (\(\Rightarrow\) is Herbrand, via Stickelberger annihilation; \(\Leftarrow\) is Ribet, constructing a nontrivial unramified extension from a congruence modular form / Galois representation.)

This is an algebra = analysis correspondence at the level of vanishing: a specific eigenspace of the class group is nonzero iff the matching zeta value is divisible by \(p\). The Main Conjecture sharpens it to an equality at the level of orders (characteristic ideals).

11.3 The Euler system of cyclotomic units

Definition 11.4 (Euler system, sketch) A compatible family \(\{c_\mathfrak{n}\}\) of elements over a family of fields \(\{F(\mathfrak{n})\}\), satisfying norm relations such as \(N_{F(\mathfrak{n}\ell)/F(\mathfrak{n})}c_{\mathfrak{n}\ell}=(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell-\ell^{\cdots})c_\mathfrak{n}\), is an Euler system.

The cyclotomic units \(\xi_a\) (Definition 4.1) form a prototypical Euler system as the conductor varies. Through Kolyvagin's derivative classes, the existence of an Euler system yields an upper bound on the size of the class (or Selmer) group, while Stickelberger gives annihilation (annihilators), which does not amount to a bound in the other direction. The two divisibilities of the Main Conjecture are carried by the Euler-system upper bound and by the analytic class number formula (Section 14.3).

Chapter 11 summary

  • The elements \(\beta\theta\in\mathbb{Z}[G]\) with \(\beta\in\mathbb{Z}[G]\) (the Stickelberger ideal \(\mathbb{Z}[G]\theta\cap\mathbb{Z}[G]\)) annihilate the class group (the algebraic shadow of \(h^-\)); the Stickelberger element \(\theta\in\mathbb{Q}[G]\) itself is the precursor of \(L_p\).
  • Herbrand–Ribet: \(A^{(1-k)}\neq0\Leftrightarrow p\mid B_k\) (vanishing correspondence).
  • The Euler system of cyclotomic units \(\to\) upper bound on class-group size (Kolyvagin–Rubin).

Chapter 12The Iwasawa Main Conjecture

The summit of the book: the algebraic characteristic ideal and the analytic \(p\)-adic \(L\) become equal in a single line.

The \(\Delta\)-eigenspace decomposition splits the Iwasawa module \(X=\bigoplus_i X^{(i)}\). The odd components (the imaginary part, \(i\) odd) correspond to the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function.

Theorem 12.1 (Iwasawa Main Conjecture — Mazur–Wiles, 1984) For the cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^\infty})\) of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)\), each odd eigencomponent \(X^{(i)}\) (\(i\) odd, \(i\not\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\)) satisfies \[ \mathrm{char}_\Lambda\bigl(X^{(i)}\bigr)=\bigl(g_{\omega^{1-i}}(T)\bigr)\subset\Lambda. \] That is, "the characteristic power series of the tower of class groups" = "the Kubota–Leopoldt \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function."

The left side is purely algebraic (the structure of the inverse limit of class groups); the right is purely analytic (\(p\)-adic interpolation of zeta values). This equality reinterprets the \(\lambda\)-invariant of Chapter 9 as "the number of zeros of the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function," and promotes the \(h^-\)/Bernoulli relation of Chapter 4 to a precise character-by-character identity over the whole tower — completing Herbrand–Ribet (vanishing) into an equality of orders. The entire journey of the book converges to this one line.

12.1 The two proofs and consequences

  • Mazur–Wiles (1984): using Galois representations on the Jacobians of the modular curves \(X_1(Np^r)\) (\(r\) varying) and the Eisenstein ideal, they construct unramified extensions (= class-group elements) matching the zeros of the \(p\)-adic \(L\), giving \(\mathrm{char}\subseteq(g)\); the reverse inclusion is the analytic class number formula (character version). Both inclusions plus finiteness give equality.
  • Kolyvagin–Rubin (Euler systems): a shorter, more algebraic proof — the Euler system of cyclotomic units gives the upper bound \(\mathrm{char}\supseteq(g)\) directly; the reverse inclusion again comes from the analytic class number formula (Section 14.3). Stickelberger annihilation only provides annihilators and cannot replace the reverse inclusion.

Consequences. The Main Conjecture describes the orders of each \(X^{(i)}\) (the growth of \(\#A_n^{(i)}\)) entirely via the zeros of \(L_p\); under Vandiver's conjecture the even part is trivial and each odd component is cyclic (Kummer theory makes \(A^{(i)}\) cyclic, hence \(X^{(i)}\) by Nakayama; having no finite submodule, \(X^{(i)}\cong\Lambda/(g_{\omega^{1-i}})\)), so the full \(\Lambda\)-module structure of the class group is determined up to isomorphism. Without that hypothesis the characteristic ideal alone does not determine the isomorphism class (\(\Lambda/(F^2)\) and \(\Lambda/(F)^{\oplus2}\) share it). It also gives the explicit identity \(v_p(h^-_p)=v_p\bigl(\prod_{\chi\neq\mathbf1\ \mathrm{even}}L_p(0,\chi)\bigr)\).

12.2 Widening horizons

The same framework "characteristic ideal of a Galois module = \(p\)-adic \(L\)" was extended repeatedly:

  • Totally real fields: Wiles (1990) over general totally real base fields.
  • Imaginary quadratic fields: Rubin (1991), via the Euler system of elliptic units. For general CM fields only conditional, partial results are known (Hida–Tilouine 1994 in the anticyclotomic case, Hsieh 2014 for one divisibility).
  • Elliptic curves: beginning with Mazur, then Coates–Wiles and Greenberg; Kato's Euler system gives one divisibility of the Main Conjecture and Skinner–Urban the reverse (equality under additional hypotheses), connecting \(L_p\) and Selmer groups — the modern route to Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer and its \(p\)-adic refinement.

Iwasawa theory is the art of reading special values of zeta functions as the geometry of a module governed by a Galois group.

Chapter 12 summary

  • Main Conjecture: \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(X^{(i)})=(g_{\omega^{1-i}})\) (Mazur–Wiles).
  • Proofs via modular curves (Eisenstein ideal) or Euler systems (Kolyvagin–Rubin).
  • Extended to totally real fields, imaginary quadratic fields, and elliptic curves (Kato + Skinner–Urban, under additional hypotheses), connecting to BSD.

Chapter 13A Modern Viewpoint: Selmer Groups and the General Main Conjecture

Recast in the language of Galois cohomology, classical Iwasawa theory (class groups & cyclotomic characters) reveals itself as one instance of a far broader framework. This chapter sketches the map.

13.1 Class groups through Galois cohomology

In cohomological terms, the class group \(A_n\) appears as the group of unramified classes inside \(H^1\). By class field theory, \(A_n\cong\mathrm{Gal}(L_n/K_n)\cong\varprojlim_m H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K_n,\mu_{p^m})^{\vee}\) (suitable dual). The Iwasawa module \(X=\varprojlim A_n\) is precisely the \(\Lambda\)-module structure of the tower's Galois cohomology \(H^1(K_\infty,\cdot)\). The facts "X is finitely generated torsion" and "control theorem" are re-proved and generalised cohomologically as the global Euler characteristic formula (Tate) and the control of cohomology (finiteness of kernel/cokernel of \(H^1(K_n)\to H^1(K_\infty)^{\Gamma_n}\); the weak Leopoldt conjecture).

Definition 13.1 (Selmer group) For a \(p\)-adic Galois representation \(V\) (with \(\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\)-action), a lattice \(T\subset V\), \(A=V/T\), the Selmer group imposes local conditions \(\{\mathcal{L}_v\}\): \[ \mathrm{Sel}(K,A)=\ker\Bigl(H^1(K,A)\to\prod_v H^1(K_v,A)/\mathcal{L}_v\Bigr). \] The Pontryagin dual \(\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Sel}(K_\infty,A)^\vee\) of the limit over the tower generalises the Iwasawa module.

The classical theory is the case \(V=\mathbb{Q}_p(1)\) (cyclotomic Tate twist), where \(\mathfrak{X}\) is essentially \(X\) (dual).

13.2 Greenberg's general Main Conjecture

Conjecture 13.2 (Greenberg, schematic) For an "ordinary" \(p\)-adic Galois representation (one with a subspace \(F^+V\subset V\) stable under the decomposition group at \(p\), inertia acting trivially on \(V/F^+V\)) for which a \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function \(L_p(V)\) can be constructed (the critical case), take the local conditions of Definition 13.1 to be the unramified classes outside \(p\) and, at \(p\), the classes coming from \(F^+A\) (the image of \(F^+V\) in \(A\)) — Greenberg's local conditions. Then the Selmer dual \(\mathfrak{X}\) is a finitely generated torsion \(\Lambda\)-module and its characteristic ideal is generated by \(L_p(V)\): \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(\mathfrak{X})=(L_p(V))\).

This extends Chapter 12 from cyclotomic characters to general motives. Known principal cases:

  • \(V=\mathbb{Q}_p(1)\) (classical): Mazur–Wiles — the main theorem of this book.
  • \(V=T_pE\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\) of an elliptic curve (ordinary reduction): Kato gives one inclusion via his Euler system (Kato's elements, \(p\)-adic Beilinson elements); Skinner–Urban the reverse, from the automorphic side — establishing the conjecture in many cases. This is the bridge \(\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\rightleftarrows\mathrm{rank}\,E(\mathbb{Q})\), a central battleground of modern number theory.
  • Modular forms / Hida families: widening the parameter \(\Lambda\) in the "weight" direction toward two-variable Iwasawa theory.

13.3 K-theory and the ETNC

From higher up, the Main Conjecture is a special case of an identity between Fitting ideals and special values. The Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture (resolved by Voevodsky–Rost) identifies algebraic K-groups \(K_{2i-1}(\mathcal{O}_K)\) with étale cohomology and expresses \(\zeta_K(1-i)\) (for \(K\) totally real and \(i\) even, up to a power of \(2\)) as a ratio of K-group orders (the Lichtenbaum conjecture). The Iwasawa Main Conjecture refines this "zeta value = cohomological invariant" over the whole tower with \(\Lambda\)-coefficients. The equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture (ETNC) unifies these, and Iwasawa theory is the central technique controlling its \(p\)-part; the Coates–Sinnott conjecture (a higher-K Stickelberger) closes the circle.

Iwasawa theory is a universal grammar translating special values of zeta into the language of cohomology.

Chapter 13 summary

  • Class groups / \(X\) recast via Galois cohomology and Selmer groups (classical = \(V=\mathbb{Q}_p(1)\)).
  • Greenberg's general Main Conjecture: \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(\mathrm{Sel}^\vee)=(L_p)\); for elliptic curves Kato + Skinner–Urban, connecting to BSD.
  • K-theory (Quillen–Lichtenbaum) and the ETNC give the final form of "zeta value = cohomology"; Iwasawa theory carries its \(p\)-part.

Proofs and Developments — To the Frontier

Chapter 14Two Proofs of the Main Conjecture

Chapter 12 stated the Main Conjecture and promised two proofs. Here is the blueprint at one more level of detail. The key is to split the equality into two divisibilities and balance them on the scale of the analytic class number formula.

14.1 Two divisibilities and the scale

For each odd \(i\not\equiv1\pmod{p-1}\), compare a generator \(f_i:=\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(X^{(i)})\) (algebraic) with \(g_i:=g_{\omega^{1-i}}\) (analytic). The proof has three parts.

  • Analytic class number formula (the scale): Iwasawa's \(p\)-adic class number formula gives equality of the \(\mu\)- and \(\lambda\)-invariants of the products over all odd components: \(\mu(\prod_i f_i)=\mu(\prod_i g_i)\), \(\lambda(\prod_i f_i)=\lambda(\prod_i g_i)\) (the formula only sees the orders at each layer, so it does not by itself give equality of the ideals). This lifts the relative class number formula \(h^-=2p\prod(-\tfrac12B_{1,\chi})\) of Chapter 4 to the whole tower.
  • One-sided divisibility (term by term): prove a divisibility in the same direction for every \(i\) (either \(f_i\mid g_i\) for all \(i\), or \(g_i\mid f_i\) for all \(i\)).
  • Conclusion: equal \(\mu,\lambda\) of the products plus same-direction divisibility at each term make the product of the quotients have \(\mu=\lambda=0\), i.e. a unit, which forces \((f_i)=(g_i)\) for all \(i\) (Exercise B-14). Mixed directions do not suffice (\(f_1=g_1h,\ g_2=f_2h\) still have equal products).

An upper bound (\(X\) small) gives \(f_i\mid g_i\); a lower bound (\(X\) large) gives \(g_i\mid f_i\). The two proofs attack from opposite poles.

14.2 Mazur–Wiles — modular curves and the Eisenstein ideal (lower bound)

The starting point is Ribet's converse to Herbrand (\(\Leftarrow\) of Theorem 11.3).

Ribet's construction. When \(p\mid B_k\), there is a cusp eigenform \(f\) congruent mod \(p\) to the weight-\(k\) Eisenstein series \(E_k\). Reducing its 2-dimensional Galois representation \(\rho_f\) mod \(p\), the congruence makes it reducible with semisimplification \(1\oplus\omega^{k-1}\) (this is all the congruence gives; the reduction itself depends on the stable lattice). Since \(\rho_f\) is irreducible (\(f\) is cuspidal), Ribet's lemma lets one choose a stable lattice whose reduction is a non-split extension with \(\mathbb{F}_p\) as subrepresentation. This yields a nontrivial extension \[ 0\to\mathbb{F}_p\to\overline\rho_f\to\mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{k-1})\to0 \] whose class is unramified outside \(p\) (\(f\) has level 1) and also at \(p\) (\(f\) is ordinary at \(p\), so \(\overline\rho_f\) restricted to the decomposition group at \(p\) has \(\mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{k-1})\) as a subrepresentation; a globally non-split extension in the opposite direction therefore splits at \(p\)). So it is an unramified class in \(H^1(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{1-k}))\), hence by class field theory a nontrivial element of \(A^{(1-k)}\). So \(p\mid B_k\Rightarrow A^{(1-k)}\neq0\).

Mazur–Wiles' lift. Lifting to the whole tower with \(\Lambda\)-coefficients: using the Jacobian of \(X_1(Np^r)\), its Hecke algebra and the Eisenstein ideal, one constructs unramified extensions (class-group elements) of multiplicity equal to the order of vanishing of \(L_p\). This gives the lower bound \(g_i\mid f_i\), i.e. \((f_i)\subseteq(g_i)\); the scale of §14.1 then yields equality (the reverse divisibility is free from the analytic formula).

14.3 Kolyvagin–Rubin — the Euler system of cyclotomic units (upper bound)

A more algebraic alternative, starting from the Euler system \(\{c_\mathfrak{n}\}\) of cyclotomic units (Chapter 11).

Kolyvagin's derivative classes. Choose auxiliary primes \(\ell\equiv1\pmod{p^M}\); for a product \(\mathfrak{n}=\ell_1\cdots\ell_r\) apply the derivative operators \(D_\mathfrak{n}=\prod_\ell D_\ell\) (each \(D_\ell=\sum_j j\,\sigma^j\), a "derivative" of the Galois sum, with \(\sigma\) a generator of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)/\mathbb{Q})\) — not the \(\sigma_\ell\) of Chapter 3) to the Euler system. Then \(D_\mathfrak{n}c_\mathfrak{n}\) is Galois-invariant mod \(p^M\) and defines a cohomology class \(\kappa_\mathfrak{n}\in H^1(\mathbb{Q},\cdot)\).

The upper-bound argument. Choosing suitable \(\ell\) by Chebotarev, these \(\kappa_\mathfrak{n}\) kill local conditions one at a time, manufacturing linear-algebra relations that annihilate each element of the class (Selmer) group. The result is that, in the even component \(j=1-i\), the class group is bounded above by units modulo cyclotomic units (the index of cyclotomic units): \[ \mathrm{char}_\Lambda\bigl(X^{(j)}\bigr)\ \big|\ \mathrm{char}_\Lambda\bigl((E_\infty/C_\infty)^{(j)}\bigr) \] (\(E_\infty,C_\infty\) the projective limits of units and cyclotomic units up the tower). Iwasawa's theorem (local units modulo cyclotomic units have characteristic ideal generated by the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function) and the class-field-theory exact sequence move this to the module unramified outside \(p\), and Kummer duality turns it into the odd component \(i\): the upper bound \(f_i\mid g_i\) (\((g_i)\subseteq(f_i)\)). With the scale, equality follows. Rubin adapted this to imaginary quadratic fields (elliptic units), proving their Main Conjecture independently.

Build from below (geometry) or annihilate from above (Euler systems): the two extremal estimates meet at one point on the analytic scale.

Chapter 14 summary

  • Main Conjecture = two divisibilities + analytic class number formula (equal \(\mu,\lambda\) of the products).
  • Mazur–Wiles: lift Ribet's construction via \(X_1(Np^r)\) / the Eisenstein ideal for the lower bound \(g_i\mid f_i\).
  • Kolyvagin–Rubin: derivative classes of the cyclotomic-unit Euler system give the upper bound \(f_i\mid g_i\).

Chapter 15Iwasawa Theory of Elliptic Curves

The Iwasawa theory of \(\mathbb{Q}_p(1)\) (class groups) is only the first example of "Iwasawa theory of \(p\)-adic Galois representations." Replacing the representation by the Tate module of an elliptic curve opens the road to Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer.

15.1 Setup

Let \(E/\mathbb{Q}\) be an elliptic curve and \(p\) a prime of good ordinary reduction (\(p\nmid a_p\)). Take \(T=T_pE=\varprojlim E[p^m]\cong\mathbb{Z}_p^2\), \(V=T\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\), \(A=E[p^\infty]=V/T\); build the Selmer group over the cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extension \(\mathbb{Q}_\infty/\mathbb{Q}\).

Definition 15.1 (Selmer group) \[ \mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_n,E[p^\infty])=\ker\Bigl(H^1(\mathbb{Q}_n,E[p^\infty])\to\prod_v\frac{H^1(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},E[p^\infty])}{\mathrm{im}\,E(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p}\Bigr). \] The dual \(\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_\infty)^\vee\) of the limit is the elliptic Iwasawa module. The fundamental exact sequence \[ 0\to E(\mathbb{Q}_n)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p\to\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_n,E[p^\infty])\to\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)[p^\infty]\to0 \] links the Mordell–Weil and Tate–Shafarevich groups.

15.2 Mazur's control theorem

Theorem 15.2 (Mazur, 1972) If \(E\) has good ordinary reduction at \(p\), the natural map \(\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_n,E[p^\infty])\to\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_\infty,E[p^\infty])^{\Gamma_n}\) has finite kernel and cokernel, uniformly bounded in \(n\). Hence \(\mathfrak{X}\) is a finitely generated \(\Lambda\)-module, and (Mazur's conjecture, known in many cases) torsion.

This is the representation-theoretic version of the control theorem \(A_n\cong X/\omega_nX\) of Chapter 9. Ordinariness is the key that makes the local condition at \(p\) compatible with the \(\Gamma\)-action. The Iwasawa invariants \(\lambda_E,\mu_E\) of \(\mathfrak{X}\) govern the growth of \(\#\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q}_n)[p^\infty]\) and the rank of \(E(\mathbb{Q}_n)\).

15.3 The \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function (Mazur–Swinnerton-Dyer)

On the analytic side: \(p\)-stabilise the modular symbols \(\{0,a/m\}\) of the weight-2 form \(f_E\) into a measure, integrate over \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\), and obtain \(L_p(E,T)\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\) (integral coefficients when ordinary). The interpolation, for finite-order \(\chi\), is \[ L_p(E,\chi)\sim(\text{Euler factor})\cdot\frac{L(E,\chi^{-1},1)}{\Omega_E}, \] \(p\)-adically interpolating the family of central values of the complex \(L\)-function.

15.4 The Coleman map and the explicit reciprocity law

Here is the technical heart sewing the two sides together. Local Galois cohomology \(H^1(\mathbb{Q}_{p,\infty},T)\) and local units (norm-compatible systems) are described as \(\Lambda\)-modules through Coleman power series. The Coates–Wiles / Perrin-Riou big exponential (régulateur \(p\)-adique) interpolates the dual exponential \(\exp^*\) and yields an explicit reciprocity law sending global classes (Kato's elements) to the \(p\)-adic \(L\):

Theorem 15.3 (Explicit reciprocity law, sketch) Perrin-Riou's map \(\mathcal{L}:H^1_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,T)\to\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\) sends the local image of Kato's Euler system to the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function \(L_p(E,T)\).

15.5 The elliptic Main Conjecture and BSD

Conjecture 15.4 (Elliptic Iwasawa Main Conjecture) \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda\bigl(\mathfrak{X}(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)\bigr)=\bigl(L_p(E,T)\bigr)\).

Under the assumptions of 15.1 alone this remains a conjecture; it is proved under additional hypotheses. The proof is two-sided as in the classical case. Kato bounded the Selmer group from above with his Euler system (Kato's elements, global classes from \(K_2\)) giving \((L_p)\subseteq\mathrm{char}\) (in \(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\); in \(\Lambda\) itself under the hypothesis that the image of the Galois action \(\rho_{E,p}:G_\mathbb{Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)\) on \(T_pE\) contains \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)\)); Skinner–Urban gave the reverse via Eisenstein series and congruences on larger groups (\(\mathrm{GU}(2,2)\)), completing equality in \(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\) when \(p\) is odd, the residual representation \(\bar\rho_{E,p}\) is irreducible, and \(\bar\rho_{E,p}\) is ramified at some prime \(q\neq p\) with \(q\Vert N_E\) (in \(\Lambda\) if moreover \(\rho_{E,p}\) is surjective; e.g. all of this holds for semistable \(E\) and \(p\ge11\)).

Connection to BSD. From the Main Conjecture (or even just one of Kato's divisibilities):

  • Rank 0: if \(L(E,1)\neq0\) then \(E(\mathbb{Q})\) is finite and \(\Sha(E)[p^\infty]\) is finite, with its \(p\)-part given by \(v_p(L(E,1)/\Omega_E)\) up to the Tamagawa numbers and torsion, as predicted by BSD (Kato's divisibility alone gives finiteness and an upper bound; the exact order uses both divisibilities) — the \(p\)-adic rank and order parts.
  • Exceptional zero: if \(E\) has split multiplicative reduction at \(p\) (\(a_p=1\)), \(L_p\) has an extra zero at the centre. The Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum conjecture predicts \(L_p'(E,1)=\mathcal{L}_p(E)\cdot L(E,1)/\Omega_E\) with \(\mathcal{L}_p(E)=\log_p q_E/\mathrm{ord}_p q_E\) (\(q_E\) the Tate period, \(\mathcal{L}_p\) the \(L\)-invariant) — proved by the two-variable theory of the next chapter.

Chapter 15 summary

  • Selmer group of \(T=T_pE\) (coefficients \(A=E[p^\infty]=V/T\), \(V=T\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\)) \(\to\) elliptic Iwasawa module \(\mathfrak{X}\), linked to Mordell–Weil and Sha.
  • Mazur's control theorem (ordinary), \(L_p(E)\) (modular symbols), the Coleman/Perrin-Riou reciprocity law.
  • Main Conjecture \(\mathrm{char}(\mathfrak{X})=(L_p(E))\) (proved under additional hypotheses by Kato + Skinner–Urban), toward \(p\)-adic BSD and the exceptional zero (\(L\)-invariant).

Chapter 16Two-variable Iwasawa Theory and Hida Families

So far one variable \(T\) (the cyclotomic direction) measured the tower. Hida built a theory letting the weight of a modular form vary as a second \(p\)-adic variable. On the plane these two span, Iwasawa theory gains new freedom.

16.1 Hida's ordinary \(\Lambda\)-adic forms

Bundle a family of cusp eigenforms into one family, varying the weight \(k\) \(p\)-adically. Hida's ordinary Hecke algebra \(\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{ord}}\) is finite flat over the weight Iwasawa algebra \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{wt}}=\mathbb{Z}_p[[W]]\), and each arithmetic point (specialisation to a weight \(k\ge2\)) yields a classical ordinary eigenform of weight \(k\). One algebraic object \(p\)-adically interpolates infinitely many modular forms.

Theorem 16.1 (Hida, big Galois representation) For each minimal prime of \(\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{ord}}\) there is a continuous \(\rho:G_\mathbb{Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathrm{Frac}\,\mathbb{I})\), \(\mathbb{I}\) finite over \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{wt}}\), with trace and determinant in \(\mathbb{I}\); a stable \(\mathbb{I}\)-lattice becomes free of rank 2 after localising at each arithmetic prime \(P\), giving a representation into \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{I}_P)\) there, and the specialisation at each arithmetic weight \(k\) (arithmetic prime \(P\)) recovers \(\rho_{f_k}\).

16.2 The two-variable Iwasawa algebra

Combining weight \(W\) and cyclotomic \(T\), use the two-variable Iwasawa algebra \(\Lambda_2=\mathbb{Z}_p[[W,T]]\) (or \(\mathbb{I}[[T]]\)). It is a 3-dimensional regular local UFD; the one-variable theory of Chapters 7–8 extends to several variables (though "pseudo-null = finite" fails and codimension is needed).

16.3 The two-variable Main Conjecture and the \(L\)-invariant

For the Selmer dual \(\mathfrak{X}_2\) of the big representation \(\rho\) (a \(\Lambda_2\)-module) and the two-variable \(p\)-adic \(L\) (Kitagawa, Greenberg–Stevens):

Conjecture 16.2 (Two-variable Main Conjecture, Greenberg) \(\mathrm{char}_{\Lambda_2}(\mathfrak{X}_2)=(L_p(W,T))\); each specialisation to an arithmetic weight \(k\) (fixing \(W\) at the value corresponding to \(k\)) is expected to be compatible with the one-variable Main Conjecture of Chapter 15, but this does not follow from the two-variable identity alone: a pseudo-null module such as \(\Lambda_2/(W,T)\) has characteristic ideal \((1)\), yet its specialisation at \(W=0\) is \(\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]/(T)\), with characteristic ideal \((T)\). One needs a control theorem for the Selmer groups (finite kernel and cokernel of the specialisation map) and the compatibility of \(L_p(W,T)\) with specialisation to obtain the one-variable identity.

Why two variables. The exceptional zero (Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum) appears as a spurious zero when looking only at \(L_p(E,T)\), but resolves naturally inside \(L_p(W,T)\). Greenberg–Stevens differentiate the two-variable \(L\) in the weight direction to derive \(\mathcal{L}_p(E)=-2\,\frac{d}{dk}\log a_p(k)\big|_{k=2}\) (\(a_p(k)\) the \(U_p\)-eigenvalue of the family as a \(p\)-adic analytic function of the weight \(k\); \(a_p(2)=1\) for split multiplicative reduction), identifying \(\mathcal{L}_p(E)=\log_p q_E/\mathrm{ord}_p q_E\) and proving MTT. What looked singular in one variable becomes a smooth derivative once a dimension is added — a hallmark of Iwasawa-theoretic thinking.

Remark 16.3 (Further horizons) Hida families are the ordinary part of the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve — a rigid-analytic family of \(p\)-adic modular forms over weight space. Galois deformation theory (Mazur) and Iwasawa theory merge here, toward measuring the deformation space itself with \(\Lambda\)-module theory (the Bloch–Kato / Fontaine–Perrin-Riou framework, the general Iwasawa–Greenberg Main Conjecture).

Chapter 16 summary

  • Hida ordinary families: weight as a second \(p\)-adic variable \(W\), big \(\rho:G_\mathbb{Q}\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathrm{Frac}\,\mathbb{I})\).
  • Two-variable Iwasawa algebra \(\Lambda_2=\mathbb{Z}_p[[W,T]]\); two-variable Main Conjecture, compatible with the one-variable one under a control theorem.
  • The exceptional zero resolves via the weight derivative; \(L\)-invariant, MTT (Greenberg–Stevens); the eigencurve and deformation theory.
Part VII
Beyond — Supersingular and Non-commutative

Chapter 17Supersingular Reduction and \(\pm\) Iwasawa Theory

Chapter 15 restricted \(p\) to good ordinary reduction. Drop that and enter supersingular reduction, and the \(\Lambda\)-module paradise — control theorem, torsionness, an integral \(p\)-adic \(L\) — collapses at once. The rescue is the \(\pm\) theory of Kobayashi–Pollack.

17.1 What breaks

If \(E/\mathbb{Q}\) has supersingular reduction at \(p\) (\(\ge5\)), then \(a_p=0\), and:

  • Two \(p\)-adic \(L\)'s, non-integral. The Frobenius eigenvalues are roots of \(X^2-a_pX+p=X^2+p\), namely \(\alpha,\beta=\pm\sqrt{-p}\), neither a unit (valuation \(\tfrac12\)). The corresponding \(L_p^\alpha,L_p^\beta\) do not lie in \(\Lambda\); their coefficients diverge at rate \(\sim n/2\) (elements of a ring larger than \(\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p\)).
  • The Selmer group is not torsion. The local condition fails to align with the \(\Gamma\)-action, so \(\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Sel}^\vee\) is not torsion over \(\Lambda\); the characteristic ideal cannot be defined directly.

17.2 \(\pm\) Selmer groups (Kobayashi, 2003)

The remedy is to change the local condition at \(p\). On the local points \(E(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p})\), define subgroups \(E^+(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p}),E^-(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p})\) controlling the "jumps" by trace (corestriction) compatibility (Kobayashi's "\(\pm\) norm groups": \(P\) lies in \(E^\pm(\mathbb{Q}_{n,p})\) when, for every \(m\le n\) of the prescribed parity — even for one sign, odd for the other; which parity goes with \(+\) depends on how the tower is indexed — the trace image drops one layer, \(\mathrm{Tr}_{n/m}P\in E(\mathbb{Q}_{m-1,p})\). The trace image does not vanish: e.g. \(P\in E(\mathbb{Q}_p)\) has \(\mathrm{Tr}_{n/m}P=p^{\,n-m}P\ne0\) in general, yet lies in both groups). Imposing these as local conditions gives \(\pm\) Selmer groups \(\mathrm{Sel}^\pm(\mathbb{Q}_\infty,E[p^\infty])\) that are \(\Lambda\)-cotorsion; their duals \(\mathfrak{X}^\pm\) are finitely generated torsion \(\Lambda\)-modules, so characteristic ideals and \(\lambda^\pm,\mu^\pm\) are restored.

17.3 \(\pm\) \(p\)-adic \(L\) (Pollack, 2003) and the \(\pm\) Main Conjecture

Pollack factored out "half-logarithm" factors \(\log_p^\pm\) (infinite products of \(\Phi_{p^n}(1+T)/p=\omega_n/(p\,\omega_{n-1})\) over even resp. odd \(n\); having infinitely many zeros, they are not elements of \(\Lambda\)) from the non-integral \(L_p^\alpha,L_p^\beta\) to extract integral half \(L\)-functions \(L_p^\pm\in\Lambda\): \[ L_p^{\alpha}=\log_p^+\!\cdot L_p^+\ \pm\ (\cdots),\qquad L_p^\pm\in\Lambda. \] Now the algebraic \(\mathfrak{X}^\pm\) and the analytic \(L_p^\pm\) live in the same ring \(\Lambda\).

Conjecture 17.1 (\(\pm\) Main Conjecture) \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda(\mathfrak{X}^\pm)=(L_p^\pm)\). Kobayashi gave one divisibility via Kato's Euler system; the rest, by Pollack–Rubin, Wan and others, holds in many cases.

The single Main Conjecture of the ordinary world splits, in the supersingular case, into two sheets, \(+\) and \(-\). This reflects the symmetric, non-unit behaviour of \(\alpha,\beta\): a discrete \(\pm\) symmetry layered over the continuous variable \(T\) — Iwasawa-theoretically beautiful.

Chapter 17 summary

  • Supersingular (\(a_p=0\)): two non-integral \(p\)-adic \(L\)'s, non-torsion Selmer — the ordinary theory collapses.
  • Kobayashi's \(\pm\) Selmer \(\mathfrak{X}^\pm\) is torsion again; Pollack's \(L_p^\pm\in\Lambda\).
  • \(\pm\) Main Conjecture \(\mathrm{char}(\mathfrak{X}^\pm)=(L_p^\pm)\): one sheet splits into two.

Chapter 18Non-commutative Iwasawa Theory

Finally, shed the one-dimensional commutative tower \(\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}_p\) itself. When the Galois group becomes a non-commutative \(p\)-adic Lie group, even "characteristic ideal" loses meaning. The new protagonist is algebraic K-theory.

18.1 Beyond commutativity

The typical non-commutative example adjoins all \(p\)-power torsion of an elliptic curve: \[ K_\infty=\mathbb{Q}(E[p^\infty]),\qquad G=\mathrm{Gal}(K_\infty/\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p) \] (for non-CM \(E\), Serre's theorem makes \(G\) an open subgroup, a 4-dimensional \(p\)-adic Lie group); or the false Tate curve extension \(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty},m^{1/p^\infty})\) (\(m>1\) a fixed integer; for \(m=1\) it degenerates to the commutative, 1-dimensional \(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})\); \(G\cong\mathbb{Z}_p\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\), 2-dimensional solvable). Their completed group algebras \(\Lambda(G)=\mathbb{Z}_p[[G]]\) are non-commutative Noetherian rings, with neither the structure theorem of Chapter 8 nor a characteristic ideal.

18.2 The K-theoretic formulation (CFKSV, 2005)

The key is to replace "characteristic ideal = determinant / characteristic polynomial" by \(K_1\), and "characteristic class" by the localisation sequence in \(K_0\). Following Coates–Fukaya–Kato–Sujatha–Venjakob: with \(H=\mathrm{Gal}(K_\infty/\mathbb{Q}_\infty^{\mathrm{cyc}})\) so that \(G/H\cong\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_p\), the canonical Ore set \[ S=\{f\in\Lambda(G):\Lambda(G)/\Lambda(G)f\ \text{is f.g. over}\ \Lambda(H)\} \] is enlarged, to allow \(p\)-power torsion, to \(S^*=\bigcup_{n\ge0}p^nS\), which localises to \(\Lambda(G)_{S^*}=\Lambda(G)_S[1/p]\). The K-theory localisation sequence \[ K_1(\Lambda(G))\to K_1(\Lambda(G)_{S^*})\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }K_0(\mathfrak{M}_H(G))\to0 \] (\(\mathfrak{M}_H(G)\) the category of finitely generated \(S^*\)-torsion \(\Lambda(G)\)-modules; \(M\in\mathfrak{M}_H(G)\iff M/M(p)\) is f.g. over \(\Lambda(H)\), \(M(p)\) the \(p\)-power torsion; \(G\) is assumed to have no element of order \(p\), and then \(\partial\) is surjective) replaces \(\mathrm{char}_\Lambda\).

Conjecture 18.1 (\(\mathrm{GL}_2\) Main Conjecture / CFKSV) (Let \(p\ge5\) and let \(E\) have good ordinary reduction at \(p\).) (i) [\(\mathfrak{M}_H(G)\) conjecture] the Selmer module \(\mathfrak{X}\) lies in \(\mathfrak{M}_H(G)\) (\(\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{X}(p)\) is f.g. over \(\Lambda(H)\)) and defines a class \([\mathfrak{X}]\in K_0(\mathfrak{M}_H(G))\). (ii) The \(p\)-adic \(L\)-function exists as an element \(\mathcal{L}\in K_1(\Lambda_A(G)_{S(A)^*})\), interpolating complex \(L\)-values through evaluation at Artin representations \(\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}_n\). Here \(A\) is the ring of integers of a finite unramified extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) (the completion at a prime above \(p\) of the maximal abelian extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\) contained in \(K_\infty\) and unramified at \(p\)), \(\Lambda_A(G)=\Lambda(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p}A\), and \(S(A)^*\) is the \(A\)-coefficient version of \(S^*\); for \(E=X_1(11)\), \(p=5\), \(A\) is the ring of integers of the unramified extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_5\) of degree 5, strictly larger than \(\mathbb{Z}_5\). (iii) The image in \(K_1(\Lambda_A(G)_{S(A)^*})\) of a characteristic element \(\xi\) of \(\mathfrak{X}\) (an element of \(K_1(\Lambda(G)_{S^*})\) with \(\partial(\xi)=[\mathfrak{X}]\)) coincides with \(\mathcal{L}\) modulo the image of \(K_1(\Lambda_A(G))\): \(\mathcal{L}\) is the characteristic element of the Selmer module, up to the coefficient extension and the ambiguity by unit classes.

18.3 Why K-theory

Over a commutative ring the determinant gave a unique "characteristic polynomial"; a non-commutative ring has no determinant — instead \(K_1(\Lambda(G))\) carries "determinant classes of units" (the Whitehead group) and \(K_0\) carries "classes of modules." The localisation sequence \(K_1\to K_1\to K_0\to0\) is the non-commutative version of the commutative divisor sequence \(\Lambda^\times\to(\mathrm{Frac}\,\Lambda)^\times\to\mathrm{Div}\to0\). Iwasawa theory is thus recast as one identity inside the localisation sequence of algebraic K-theory, merging completely with the Burns–Flach ETNC and the Fukaya–Kato framework.

The characteristic polynomial goes to \(K_1\), the characteristic ideal to \(K_0\): the final form of Iwasawa theory lives in the localisation sequence of K-theory.

The story of "zeta = characteristic class," begun with curves over finite fields (the Interlude), reaches here its most abstract language — algebraic K-theory. The commutative cyclotomic tower was the clearest gateway into that grand structure.

Appendix AThe Case \(p=2\)

The text fixed \(p\) odd. For \(p=2\) the following adjustments are needed. (i) \((\mathbb{Z}/2^k)^\times\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2^{k-2}\) (\(k\ge3\)) is not cyclic, so \(\mathbb{Z}_2^\times\cong\{\pm1\}\times(1+4\mathbb{Z}_2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}_2\), with Teichmüller part \(\{\pm1\}\). (ii) The isomorphism \(\log:1+p\mathbb{Z}_p\cong p\mathbb{Z}_p\) must be shrunk to \(1+4\mathbb{Z}_2\cong4\mathbb{Z}_2\) (\(\log_2\) still converges on \(1+2\mathbb{Z}_2\), but \(\log_2(-1)=0\), and \(\exp\) converges only on \(4\mathbb{Z}_2\)). (iii) The cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-extension is the real part \(\bigcup\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^n})^+\) of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^\infty})\) (the tower \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}),\dots\)). The structure theorem and the class number formula \(e_n=\mu2^n+\lambda n+\nu\) hold verbatim, and Ferrero–Washington (\(\mu=0\)) holds for abelian fields at \(p=2\) as well.

Appendix BExercises and Solutions

Hands-on checks of the main results. Solutions expand under ▸ Solution.

B-1. Compute \(\Phi_{12}(X)\) and list the subfields of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})\) matched to subgroups of \((\mathbb{Z}/12)^\times\).

Solution

\(\Phi_{12}(X)=X^4-X^2+1\) (\(\varphi(12)=4\)). \((\mathbb{Z}/12)^\times=\{1,5,7,11\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2\); the three order-2 subgroups \(\langle\sigma_5\rangle,\langle\sigma_7\rangle,\langle\sigma_{11}\rangle\) fix the quadratic fields \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) (\(\sigma_5\) fixes \(i=\zeta_{12}^{3}\) since \(5\equiv1\bmod4\)), \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})\) (\(\sigma_7\) fixes \(\zeta_3=\zeta_{12}^{4}\) since \(7\equiv1\bmod3\)), and \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)\) (\(\sigma_{11}=\sigma_{-1}\), complex conjugation, fixes the real subfield), respectively. So \(\mathbb{Q}\subset\{\mathbb{Q}(i),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)\}\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})\).

B-2. Show \(\xi_a=\tfrac{1-\zeta_p^a}{1-\zeta_p}\) is a unit, and check \(\xi_a\xi_b\equiv\xi_{ab}\pmod{(1-\zeta_p)}\).

Solution

Unit: Proposition 2.3. Congruence: \(\xi_a=1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{a-1}\equiv a\pmod{(1-\zeta)}\) (each \(\zeta^j\equiv1\)); likewise \(\xi_b\equiv b\), \(\xi_{ab}\equiv ab\). So \(\xi_a\xi_b\equiv ab\equiv\xi_{ab}\).

B-3. Using Theorem 3.3, derive quadratic reciprocity from \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q^*})\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)\) (\(q^*=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}q\)).

Solution (outline)

The quadratic Gauss sum \(g=\sum_a(\tfrac aq)\zeta_q^a\) satisfies \(g^2=q^*\), so \(\mathbb{Q}(g)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q^*})\) is the unique quadratic subfield of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)\). For \(\ell\neq q\), \(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell=\sigma_\ell\) is trivial on \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q^*})\) iff \(\ell\) is a square mod \(q\), i.e. \((\tfrac\ell q)=1\). On the other hand \(\sigma_\ell\) sends \(\sqrt{q^*}\mapsto(\tfrac{q^*}\ell)\sqrt{q^*}\). Equating gives \((\tfrac\ell q)=(\tfrac{q^*}\ell)\), whence, for odd \(\ell\neq q\), \((\tfrac\ell q)(\tfrac q\ell)=(-1)^{\frac{\ell-1}2\frac{q-1}2}\).

B-4. Verify \(\#(\Lambda/(p^m,\omega_n))=p^{mp^n}\) and re-derive the \(\mu p^n\) term of Theorem 9.6.

Solution

By Lemma 9.3, \(\omega_n\) is distinguished of degree \(p^n\), so \((\mathbb{Z}/p^m)[T]/(\omega_n)\) is free of rank \(p^n\), of order \(p^{mp^n}\). Summing over the \(p\)-torsion pieces gives \(\mu=\sum m_i\), contributing \(\mu p^n\).

B-5. For distinguished irreducible \(P\) (degree \(d\)) with \(P\nmid\omega_n\) for all \(n\), show \(\#(\Lambda/(P,\omega_n))=p^{dn+c}\) (large \(n\)) by valuation of roots.

Solution

By Lemma 9.4, \(\#=p^{v_p(\det\omega_n|\Lambda/(P))}\), \(\det=\prod_{P(\alpha)=0}\omega_n(\alpha)\); each \(v_p(\omega_n(\alpha))=n+c_\alpha\) for \(n\gg0\), and summing over the \(d\) roots gives \(dn+c\).

B-6. \(p=37\) is irregular (\(37\mid B_{32}\)). Which eigencomponent is nontrivial, by Herbrand–Ribet?

Solution

\(k=32\) (even, \(2\le32\le34=p-3\)). By Theorem 11.3, \(A^{(1-32)}=A^{(-31)}=A^{(5)}\) (mod 36: \(1-32\equiv5\)) is nontrivial; i.e. the \(\omega^5\)-eigencomponent of \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{37}))[37]\) is \(\neq0\).

B-7. Deduce that \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\) is a UFD from the Weierstrass preparation theorem.

Solution (outline)

Any \(0\neq f=p^\mu Pu\) (Theorem 7.5). \(p\) is prime (\(\Lambda/(p)=\mathbb{F}_p[[T]]\) is a domain). A distinguished \(P\) is monic in \(\mathbb{Z}_p[T]\), so factors uniquely there (a UFD), with distinguished irreducible factors. The irreducibles of \(\Lambda\) are exactly \(p\) and distinguished irreducible polynomials, and the factorisation is unique up to units. Hence \(\Lambda\) is a UFD.

B-8. (Ch. 5) Show the finite-order elements of \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\) are exactly \(\mu_{p-1}\) (\(p\) odd).

Solution

\(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\cong\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p)\) (Theorem 5.3). \(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p\cong(\mathbb{Z}_p,+)\) is torsion-free (the \(p\)-adic log is an isomorphism and \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) is torsion-free), so the only torsion lives in \(\mu_{p-1}\).

B-9. (Ch. 5) Under the Iwasawa isomorphism \(\mu\mapsto\int(1+T)^xd\mu\), check that the Dirac measure \(\delta_a\) corresponds to \((1+T)^a\), a power series for \(a\in\mathbb{Z}\).

Solution

\(\int(1+T)^x\,d\delta_a(x)=(1+T)^a\). For \(a\ge0\) this is a polynomial; for \(a\lt 0\), \((1+T)^{-1}=\sum(-T)^k\) etc. give a power series in \(\Lambda\). For general \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_p\), \((1+T)^a=\sum\binom ak T^k\in\Lambda\) (\(\binom ak\in\mathbb{Z}_p\)).

B-10. (Ch. 7) Show \(\omega_n=(1+T)^{p^n}-1\) is distinguished of degree \(p^n\), and that \(\omega_n/\omega_{n-1}\) is distinguished of degree \(p^n-p^{n-1}\).

Solution

\((1+T)^{p^n}\equiv1+T^{p^n}\pmod p\) (Frobenius), so \(\omega_n\equiv T^{p^n}\): leading coefficient 1, the rest divisible by \(p\). With \(Y=\omega_{n-1}\), \(\omega_n=(Y+1)^p-1=\sum_{j=1}^p\binom pjY^j=Y\bigl(\binom p1+\cdots+Y^{p-1}\bigr)\), so \(\omega_n/\omega_{n-1}=\sum_{j=1}^p\binom pj Y^{j-1}\equiv Y^{p-1}\pmod p\): distinguished of degree \((p-1)p^{n-1}=p^n-p^{n-1}\).

B-11. (Ch. 8–9) For \(M=\Lambda/(T-p)\), compute \(\#(M/\omega_nM)\) and read off \(\lambda,\mu\).

Solution

\(P=T-p\) is distinguished irreducible (degree 1), \(M\cong\mathbb{Z}_p\) (send \(T\mapsto p\)), and \(\omega_n\) acts as multiplication by \((1+p)^{p^n}-1\). By Lemma 9.4, \(\#=p^{v_p((1+p)^{p^n}-1)}=p^{n+1}\) (since \(v_p(p)=1>\tfrac1{p-1}\), each \(p\)-power adds \(1\), starting from \(v_p((1+p)-1)=1\)). So \(\lambda=\deg P=1\), \(\mu=0\), \(\nu=1\).

B-12. (Ch. 9) Derive the control-theorem commutator \([\tau,x]=\omega_nx\) from \(\tau=\sigma^{p^n}\) acting on \(X\) as \((1+T)^{p^n}\) (\(\sigma\mapsto\gamma_0=1+T\)).

Solution

\(X\) is abelian (write additively); \([\tau,x]=\tau x\tau^{-1}-x\). The conjugation action of \(\tau\) is the module action \((1+T)^{p^n}\cdot\), so \(\tau x\tau^{-1}=(1+T)^{p^n}x\), whence \([\tau,x]=((1+T)^{p^n}-1)x=\omega_nx\).

B-13. (Ch. 10) In \(L_p(1-n,\chi)=-(1-\chi\omega^{-n}(p)p^{n-1})B_{n,\chi\omega^{-n}}/n\), observe the Euler factor and the case \(\chi=\mathbf1\), \(n=1\).

Solution

For \(\chi=\mathbf1,n=1\): \(\omega^{-1}\) has conductor \(p\), so \(\omega^{-1}(p)=0\) and the factor is \((1-0)=1\); \(L_p(0,\mathbf1)=-B_{1,\omega^{-1}}\) is finite, but with \(v_p=-1\) (a \(p\) in the denominator). This is the trace of \(\zeta_p(s)=L_p(s,\mathbf1)\) having a pole at \(s=1\) (like the complex \(\zeta\)): the trivial-character \(p\)-adic zeta is not analytic at \(s=1\), while \((s-1)L_p(s,\mathbf1)\) is.

B-14. (Ch. 14) From "equal \(\mu\)- and \(\lambda\)-invariants of the products (\(\mu(\prod f_i)=\mu(\prod g_i)\), \(\lambda(\prod f_i)=\lambda(\prod g_i)\)) and one-sided divisibility \(f_i\mid g_i\) (\(f_i\neq0\)) for each \(i\)" deduce \((f_i)=(g_i)\) for all \(i\) (use the Weierstrass preparation theorem).

Solution

Each \(f_i\mid g_i\) gives \(g_i=f_ih_i\) with \(h_i\in\Lambda\); the product gives \(\prod g_i=(\prod f_i)(\prod h_i)\). Since \(\mu\) and \(\lambda\) are additive under products (multiply the Weierstrass factorisations \(p^\mu PU\)), \(\mu(\prod h_i)=\lambda(\prod h_i)=0\); all are non-negative, so \(\mu(h_i)=\lambda(h_i)=0\) for each \(i\). By Weierstrass preparation an element with \(\mu=\lambda=0\) is a unit, so each \(h_i\) is a unit and \((f_i)=(g_i)\).

B-15. (Ch. 15) Using \(0\to E(\mathbb{Q}_n)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p\to\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_n)\to\Sha[p^\infty]\to0\), explain why \(L(E,1)\neq0\) (so \(\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q})\) finite by the Main Conjecture) forces \(\mathrm{rank}\,E(\mathbb{Q})=0\).

Solution

If \(\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q},E[p^\infty])\) is finite, so is its subgroup \(E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p\). But \(E(\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}^r\oplus(\text{finite})\) gives \(E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p\cong(\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)^r\), infinite if \(r>0\). Hence \(r=0\) (and \(\Sha[p^\infty]\) finite). This is the \(p\)-adic shadow of BSD's "\(L(E,1)\neq0\Rightarrow\mathrm{rank}=0\)."

F.1 Cyclotomic polynomials and degrees (Ch. 2)

F-1. Compute \(\Phi_8,\Phi_9,\Phi_{10}\).

Solution

\(\Phi_8=x^4+1\), \(\Phi_9=x^6+x^3+1\), \(\Phi_{10}=x^4-x^3+x^2-x+1\). Each comes from dividing \(X^n-1\) by the product of lower \(\Phi_d\).

F-2. Show \(\Phi_{2m}(x)=\Phi_m(-x)\) for odd \(m>1\).

Solution

For odd \(m\) the primitive \(2m\)-th roots are the \(-\zeta\) (\(\zeta\) primitive \(m\)-th), so \(\Phi_{2m}(x)=\prod(x+\zeta)=(-1)^{\varphi(m)}\Phi_m(-x)\); \(\varphi(m)\) is even (\(m>2\)), so the sign is \(+\).

F-3. Using \(\deg\Phi_n=\varphi(n)\), find \([\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{20}):\mathbb{Q}]\) and \([\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{20}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{20})^+]\).

Solution

\(\varphi(20)=\varphi(4)\varphi(5)=8\); \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{20})^+\) has index 2, so \([\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{20}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{20})^+]=2\) and \([\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{20})^+:\mathbb{Q}]=4\).

F.2 Ramification and splitting (Ch. 2–3)

F-4. Write the factorisation of \(5\) in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)\) (\(\lambda=1-\zeta_5\)). What is \(N(\lambda)\)?

Solution

By Proposition 2.3, \((5)=(1-\zeta_5)^4\) (totally ramified, \(\varphi(5)=4\)), and \(N(\lambda)=\Phi_5(1)=5\).

F-5. Into how many primes does \(2\) split in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)\)? Residue degree?

Solution

\(2\nmid7\), unramified. The order of \(2\) in \((\mathbb{Z}/7)^\times\) is \(3\) (\(2,4,1\)), so \(f=3\), \(g=6/3=2\): two primes of residue degree \(3\).

F-6. State the condition for a prime \(p\) to split completely in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)\) (\(p\nmid n\)).

Solution

\(f=1\Leftrightarrow p\equiv1\pmod n\); then it splits into \(g=\varphi(n)\) primes (Theorem 3.3).

F-7. List the three quadratic subfields of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})\) with their subgroups of \((\mathbb{Z}/12)^\times\).

Solution

\((\mathbb{Z}/12)^\times=\{1,5,7,11\}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2\); the order-2 subgroups \(\langle5\rangle,\langle7\rangle,\langle11\rangle\) fix \(\mathbb{Q}(i),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)\) (cf. B-1).

F.3 Units, Bernoulli, class numbers (Ch. 4)

F-8. Compute \(B_1,\dots,B_4\) and verify \(\zeta(-1)=-B_2/2\).

Solution

\(B_1=-\tfrac12,B_2=\tfrac16,B_3=0,B_4=-\tfrac1{30}\). Indeed \(\zeta(-1)=-1/12=-B_2/2\) (\(\zeta(1-n)=-B_n/n\), \(n=2\)).

F-9. Is \(p=11\) regular? Check the numerators of \(B_2,\dots,B_8\) mod 11.

Solution

Numerators \(1,-1,1,-1\) (absolute value 1), none divisible by 11. So \(11\) is regular (the first irregular prime is \(37\)). Check with tool ②.

F-10. Expand \(\xi_2=\tfrac{1-\zeta_5^2}{1-\zeta_5}\) in \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_5]\) and show it is a unit.

Solution

\(\xi_2=1+\zeta_5\), with inverse \(\tfrac{1}{1+\zeta_5}\); since \(2\cdot3\equiv1\pmod5\) this is also an algebraic integer, so \(\xi_2\) is a unit (Proposition 2.3, B-2).

F.4 \(p\)-adic numbers (Ch. 5)

F-11. Is \(\sqrt2\in\mathbb{Q}_7\)? Decide by Hensel and find the first digits.

Solution

\(3^2=9\equiv2\pmod7\), a simple root (\(f'(3)=6\not\equiv0\)), so Hensel lifts \(\sqrt2\in\mathbb{Z}_7\). Iterating, \(\sqrt2=3+1\cdot7+2\cdot7^2+\cdots\) (\(\equiv108\bmod343\); check \(108^2\equiv2\)).

F-12. Find the first digits of the Teichmüller value \(\omega(3)\in\mathbb{Z}_7\) (an element of \(\mu_6\)).

Solution

\(\omega(3)=\lim 3^{7^m}\equiv3\pmod7\), \(\omega(3)^6=1\); numerically \(\omega(3)=3+4\cdot7+6\cdot7^2+\cdots\).

F-13. Using \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\cong\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb{Z}_p)\), count the order-4 elements of \(\mathbb{Z}_5^\times\).

Solution

The order-4 elements are the generators of \(\mu_4=\mu_{p-1}\) (\(p=5\)), exactly \(\varphi(4)=2\); the torsion-free \(1+5\mathbb{Z}_5\) contributes none.

F.5 \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-extensions, \(\omega_n\) (Ch. 6–7)

F-14. Expand \(\omega_1=(1+T)^p-1\) for \(p=3\) and verify it is distinguished.

Solution

\((1+T)^3-1=T^3+3T^2+3T\): leading coefficient 1, others divisible by 3; distinguished of degree 3 (\(\equiv T^3\bmod3\)).

F-15. What is the degree of the first layer \(\mathbb{Q}_1\) of the \(\mathbb{Z}_3\)-extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\)? How does \(3\) ramify?

Solution

\(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_1/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\), degree 3 (\(=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_9)^+\)). By Theorem 6.2, \(3\) is totally ramified (\(e=3\)), the only ramified prime.

F-16. Check that \(T\) and \(p\) are coprime primes in \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\) and identify \(\Lambda/(p,T)\).

Solution

\((p)\) and \((T)\) are distinct height-1 primes (\(\Lambda/(p)=\mathbb{F}_p[[T]]\), \(\Lambda/(T)=\mathbb{Z}_p\), both domains); \(\Lambda/(p,T)=\mathbb{F}_p\), the unique height-2 prime \(\mathfrak m=(p,T)\).

F.6 Elliptic Iwasawa labs (SageMath)

Chapters 15 & 17 on the computer. Outputs vary slightly with version; use these to check the ideas. Tool ⑤ also gives \(a_p\) and the reduction type.

F-17. For E = EllipticCurve('11a1'), compute \(a_5\) and decide the reduction type at \(p=5\).

Solution
sage: E = EllipticCurve('11a1')
sage: E.ap(5)
1
sage: E.is_ordinary(5), E.is_supersingular(5)
(True, False)

\(a_5=1\), \(5\nmid1\), hence ordinary; the good-ordinary theory of Chapter 15 applies directly.

F-18. Form the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-series at \(p=5\) and read off how to extract \(\mu,\lambda\).

Solution
sage: L = E.padic_lseries(5)
sage: L.series(4)        # T-adic power series (5-adic coefficients)
O(5^...) + ... *T + ... *T^2 + ...

For \(L_p(E,T)=\sum c_iT^i\): \(\mu=\min_i v_5(c_i)\) and \(\lambda\) is the \(T\)-degree where the first unit coefficient of \(5^{-\mu}L_p\) appears. For 11a1 at \(p=5\) (rank 0, \(L(E,1)\neq0\)) the constant term \(5+4\cdot5^2+\cdots\) is not a unit, and in fact \(\mu=1,\ \lambda=0\) (LMFDB): \(L(E,1)\neq0\) alone does not force \(\mu=\lambda=0\) (here \(p=5\) is anomalous, \(a_5=1\)).

F-19. For the rank-1 curve '37a1', observe the analytic rank and the vanishing of \(L_p\) at the centre.

Solution
sage: E = EllipticCurve('37a1'); E.rank(), E.analytic_rank()
(1, 1)
sage: L = E.padic_lseries(5); L.series(4)

Rank 1 means \(L(E,1)=0\), and correspondingly \(L_p(E,T)\) vanishes at \(T=0\) (\(\lambda\ge1\)); Iwasawa-theoretically the Selmer group has positive \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-rank, i.e. \(T\) divides the characteristic power series of \(\mathfrak{X}\).

F-20. With tool ⑤ or Sage, compute \(a_p\) of \(y^2=x^3-x\) (EllipticCurve([-1,0])) for \(p=3,5,7,13\) and state the supersingular rule.

Solution
sage: E = EllipticCurve([-1,0])    # y^2 = x^3 - x, CM by Z[i]
sage: [(p, E.ap(p), E.is_supersingular(p)) for p in [3,5,7,13]]
[(3,0,True),(5,-2,False),(7,0,True),(13,6,False)]

\(a_p=0\) (supersingular) for \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), ordinary for \(p\equiv1\pmod4\): the effect of CM by \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) (supersingular iff \(p\) is inert in \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\)). The supersingular case needs the \(\pm\) theory of Chapter 17.

F-21. In one sentence, why does \(L(E,1)\neq0\Rightarrow E(\mathbb{Q})\) finite (one side of BSD) follow from one divisibility of Kato's Main Conjecture?

Solution

Kato's Euler system bounds the Selmer group from above so that \(\mathrm{char}(\mathfrak{X})\) contains \(L_p\) (the argument of B-15): \(L(E,1)\neq0\Rightarrow L_p(E,0)\neq0\Rightarrow\) Selmer finite \(\Rightarrow E(\mathbb{Q})\) finite (rank 0).

F-22. (Supersingular) Tool ⑤ shows \(y^2=x^3-x\) is supersingular at \(p=7\) (\(a_7=0\)). Build the \(p\)-adic \(L\)-series in Sage and say what differs from the ordinary case.

Solution
sage: E = EllipticCurve([-1,0])      # y^2 = x^3 - x
sage: E.ap(7), E.is_supersingular(7)
(0, True)
sage: L = E.padic_lseries(7)          # supersingular object
sage: L.series(3)                     # coefficients in Q_7(√-7), non-integral

Unlike the ordinary case, the Frobenius eigenvalues \(\alpha,\beta=\pm\sqrt{-7}\) are non-units, so \(L_p\) does not collect into a single integral series; the \(7\)-adic valuations of its coefficients are unbounded below (the value at layer \(n\) carries a factor \(\alpha^{-n}\), so the denominators grow like \(7^{n/2}\)) — it lies outside \(\Lambda\). As in Chapter 17, the integral \(L_7^\pm\in\Lambda\) are not obtained by dividing this single series by \(\log_7^\pm\): pair it with its partner \(L_7^\beta\) for \(\beta=-\alpha\) (conjugate the coefficients under \(\alpha\mapsto-\alpha\)); since \(L_7^\alpha=\log_7^+L_7^++\alpha\log_7^-L_7^-\) and \(L_7^\beta=\log_7^+L_7^+-\alpha\log_7^-L_7^-\), divide the sum by \(2\log_7^+\) and the difference by \(2\alpha\log_7^-\). The resulting \(L_7^\pm\) are paired with the \(\pm\) Selmer groups.

F-23. (Supersingular) How does the \(\pm\) Main Conjecture \(\mathrm{char}(\mathfrak{X}^\pm)=(L_p^\pm)\) differ from the ordinary one (Ch. 15) in "number of variables" and "number of invariants"?

Solution

Both use one cyclotomic variable \(T\) (\(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]\)), but supersingular splits the conjecture into two sheets \(+\) and \(-\), with two pairs of invariants \((\lambda^+,\mu^+)\) and \((\lambda^-,\mu^-)\). The symmetry of \(\alpha,\beta\) (\(a_p=0\)) appears as this discrete \(\pm\) split; in the ordinary case \(\alpha\) is a unit and there is a single sheet.

The Main Conjecture, made visible by computation The \(\lambda^-\) returned by tool ② is a lower bound from the count of irregular indices; it equals the \(\lambda\)-invariant of the analytic \(p\)-adic \(L\)-functions \(L_p(s,\omega^{\,\mathrm{even}})\) for every \(p\) checked so far (all \(p\lt 2^{31}\), Hart–Harvey–Ong), though this is not known in general. What Hart–Harvey–Ong actually compute, for each irregular pair, is a test of the same kind as the one tool ② performs — a congruence between Bernoulli numbers modulo \(p^2\) (\(\Delta\not\equiv0\Leftrightarrow\lambda=1\)) — i.e. that the analytic \(\lambda\) equals this count; the statement in their paper that the class-group invariant satisfies \(\lambda_p=i_p\) follows from this by the Main Conjecture together with Vandiver's conjecture, which they verify in the same range. The Iwasawa Main Conjecture (Theorem 12.1) asserts that this equals the algebraic \(\lambda\) — that of the characteristic power series of the class-group tower \(X^{(i)}\). So the number of zeros counted from zeta in ② is also the slope \(\lambda\) of the actual class-number growth \(e_n=\mu p^n+\lambda n+\nu\). Note, however, that tool ④ is a model: it plots the formula with the \(\lambda\) you type in and computes no class groups. Feeding ②'s value into ④ and seeing the same slope is a repetition of the input, not a numerical test of the Main Conjecture; to see the equality in numbers one must compute the class groups of the layers independently and compare them with the count from ②.
